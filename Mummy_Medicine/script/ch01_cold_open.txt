In 1903, the German drug company Merck printed its price list. Page after page of drugs and chemicals. And right under the musk, there's this line. *Mumia vera aegyptiaca.* Genuine Egyptian mummy. Seventeen marks fifty a kilo. *While supplies last.*

Supplies lasted. In 1924, two years after Tutankhamun's tomb was found, it was still in the catalog.

For centuries, Europeans ate mummies as medicine. Kings did it. Scientists did it. And when the real mummies ran out, they didn't stop. They found fresher ones.

So here's the question. How did eating the dead become medicine? And how did it last until 1924?

It starts with a black, sticky rock.
