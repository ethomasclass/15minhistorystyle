Then some doctors decided Egypt was overrated anyway.

A popular theory said a body's healing power was strongest in someone young and healthy who died suddenly. By that logic, a fresh body beat a three-thousand-year-old pharaoh. So in 1609, a German chemist named Oswald Croll published a recipe for making your own.

Take the body of a red-haired man. Twenty-four years old. Hanged, or broken on the wheel. Leave him out for a day and a night, under the sun and the moon. Cut the flesh into small pieces. Soak them in myrrh, aloe and spirit of wine. Dry.

That's a real recipe. It was printed in a medical book, and other doctors copied it for decades.

Why red hair? Because a red-haired man's blood was supposed to be thinner, and his flesh better. Which, honestly, is the least disturbing part of that sentence.

And mummy was only the beginning.
