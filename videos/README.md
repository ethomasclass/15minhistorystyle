## Your standing preferences (from your notes across the videos)

**Script**
- **Written for a general audience, easy to follow** (from *Dressed to Kill*). Learn from what Vox explainers do well, don't copy
  their look: see the skill's `references/explainer-craft.md`. One name per thing, terms defined where they appear, a confusion pass
  before voicing.
- **Hook-first cold opens** (from *Dressed to Kill*, after the retention graphs): the first sentence is the hook, never a date; the
  thumbnail's image is on screen at frame 0; the question by about 0:40; one or two promises that pay off later. The channel intro
  at about 1:00 is fine (no cliff there in *King Andrew*'s graph). Promoted views skip fast, so judge retention on organic traffic.
- **Write for someone who knows nothing** (from *The Balloon War*): no assumed background, connect to something today, sound like
  talking. Build the script on **story loops** (open a question, close it later), and leave a one-second beat after the cold open.
- No historian names in the narration ("historians still argue" is fine). No modern politicians. No implied link to today's parties.
- Present what supporters and critics each saw, and let the ending explain both readings rather than deliver a verdict.
- Required concepts get named and defined, and the cause gets said plainly (e.g. "expanded suffrage is why he won").
- Open on a real document or artifact. Trim to time with a cut list.
- **Every video has two narrated subscribe reminders** (from *Say It Ain't So* on): one short plug (from *The Exact Spot* on: about **two-thirds** of the way in, after the story's big build, not a third in where it kills momentum), tied to the topic
  with a wry twist (Ouija: the board spells it; Black Sox: "unlike William Wansley, nobody's paying me to say it"), and a one-line
  thank-you and subscribe over the end screen, after the closing line has landed. The like/subscribe line stays in the description too.

**Picture**
- **Photos have no frames and always look worn** (Oct 2026): no white borders or card mounts; every archival photo is cropped out of its mount and shown as a creased old print (fold lines, a web of fine cracks, chipped and torn edges with white paper fibres, faint stains) with `tools/crease.py` (in `projects/Reconstruction/tests/tools/` until it moves into the template).
- **The collage board in every video** (from the Reconstruction series, Oct 2026): each chapter pins one piece to a board on the desk, teal yarn with *but* / *therefore* tags ties causes to effects, and the ending pulls back to the whole board. You chose the board (cut-paper collage in the channel's colours, with the tilted desk for moving to the maps) over the block-in-the-wall "chunk wall". No case-file or crime language on it. Spec: [`../techniques/collage_board.md`](../techniques/collage_board.md).
- **Cut-outs on the notebook, as many photos as possible** (*The Balloon War*). Each photo's subject is cut out and placed on the
  dark ruled paper, or the photo lands as a cream card on the desk. **No full-screen photos with slow pushes ("Ken Burns"), no 3D
  photo fly-throughs, no color photos** (modern ones go black and white too).
- **Every portrait is a full bust cut-out** (West Ep 2, Oct 2026): any picture of one person shows just the head and shoulders, cut out of
  the page onto the desk with a soft contact shadow: no paper print, no photo background, no circle mount, no oval or poster frame.
  The beat's subject gets the see-through coral tint and teal trace; anyone else stays plain black and white; quiet sections, no
  coral. Group photos and scenes stay as prints (and photos of Native people stay plain prints, never cut out).
- **White things are cut out against black** (West Ep 2): a snow drift drawn on white came out ragged; make white props (snow,
  paper, bone) on a black background and mask against it (`tools/mask_white.py --bg=black`).
- **Text never runs off the frame.** You caught this in Fix Everything, King Andrew, Grip Tighter and Ouija. Run the probe every time.
- **Notes on paper slips, inside the safe margin** (Reconstruction, Oct 2026): handwriting over a busy picture was hard to read, so notes sit on torn paper slips (dark ink on cream; pink for the subject). Keep titles and notes out of the outer 5% too (`SAFE=96` in the text probe).
- **Say fractions so they can't be misheard** (Reconstruction ch06): "three-quarters of the states", not "three out of four states", which sounds like there were four.
- Big titles stay on screen for at least 2 seconds.
- **Gentle transitions** (American West Ep 1, Oct 2026): no whip pans. A "whip" shot change is a soft drift (a ~half-second
  crossfade with a 70 px slide and light blur), a "cut" is a 6-frame dissolve (`Shots` in `src/previews/fx.tsx`); the whip sound
  plays at half level.
- **Chunk History branding** (Oct 2026): the 3D Chunk intro after the cold open (5.5 s) and a 2 s mark snap between chapters (the
  coral quarter swings in and locks), from `brand/chunk-history/` (`ep1/src/kit/ChunkIntro.tsx`). **Its sound is clicks and page
  turns only: no tones, no music** (you tried a marimba tune, felt piano, a chopped horn and lo-fi and chose this).
- **Gentle chapter endings** (from *The Exact Spot*): about 2 seconds of hold after the last word, the music lifting into it, then a slower dip to black before the logo break. No cutting off right after the voice.
- **A built-in end screen** (about 20 s) with clean slots for YouTube's end-screen elements.
- **Maps:** our own drawn map in 3D perspective (tilted, regions rising as slabs, standing labels), not terrain.
- No black edges, and the camera never shows past the edge of a map or stage.
- Drawn diagrams and cards beat fussy illustrated props. **If an image can be misread, replace it with a drawn diagram** (The Exact Spot: soldiers "inside a fence" became a drawn trap beside the map).
- **Masks are checked at full resolution** (`tools/mask_closeup.py`), not just on the contact sheet. A rough edge gets cleaned or hand-traced before the scene ships.
- **Heads stay in frame** through every push and drift. Check the first and last frame of each push into a group.
- **Make an allusion explicit.** When a map points to a later event (the 1861 split), label it on screen: sides, a key, a dated pin, the event's name.
- No caricatured engravings in a serious chapter.
- **No on-screen tag on AI images, and never "AI" on screen** (West series, Oct 2026). Archival images keep their source tags; AI
  images are recorded only in the credits file and prompt log.
- No "drawn for this video" tags, no end credits, no mention of ElevenLabs on screen, and no "presents" line on the title.
- **Notes are bare teal handwriting, no tape or strip behind them** (from the West series, Oct 2026; replaces the backed
  notes used in the Reconstruction videos). Orange tape is for titles only.
- **Everything new stays on the black desk** (from the West series): no cream or white cards, mounts, tags or sticker borders. The
  only light tones on screen are the archival scans themselves. New devices (ledger cards, circle mounts, map cards, year stamps) are
  dark paper with teal rules and coral/orange accents.
- **Cut-outs** (people and objects, collage-style): no white sticker edge; a hard ink-black offset shadow, grain over; the teal
  trace is the edge when it's the subject; one coral subject per frame. **In chapters about Native nations, people stay in their
  own photographs** (full frame or a circle mount), never cut out and arranged as collage pieces; objects only.
- **Depth, not flat paper** (your West-series note: "feels a little flat… no depth variation or texture"): wide shot then a detail
  cut-in for every beat, a cut every 3–6 s, never one composition for more than ~7 s; one camera move per shot and never the same
  move twice in a row (push, pull, pan, tilt, parallax, static on the payoff); deep zooms only on high-resolution scans; a desk with
  real depth (cards at different heights, a lamp pool, slight focus fall-off, contact shadows); pieces land with weight (slap,
  slight overshoot, a small decaying shake) and settled pieces breathe; texture on everything (torn-paper fibre edges, translucent
  tape, stains, halftone).
- **3D where it explains something** (you loved the tilted 3D township): the land-grant checkerboard rising as slabs, the race as an
  elevation ribbon. Drawn teal 3D, never photoreal. Say "heights exaggerated" on screen when they are.
- **Trace and source, don't eyeball:** diagrams traced from real drawings, maps from real data, document swipes placed from the
  scan's actual line positions (Gemini's line finder, `review/ai_tests/assets/act_lines.json` in the West Ep 1 project).
- **Hook alignment in the cold open** (Kallaway): the picture, the first spoken line and a short written hook (orange tape matching
  the thumbnail) all say the same thing within the first ~2 seconds.

**Neutral on today's companies and people**

  official terms, don't name people who haven't been convicted, say "charged" and "pleaded not guilty", keep a scandal and a company in
  separate sentences unless they're connected, and give critics' views as critics' views. Date anything that can change ("when this

**Images**
- **AI images are a per-video call.** Black Sox used none ("roll with what is available"); Grip Tighter used none; Ouija used many.

- **Masks must look clean on AI images too.** Check the subject and background layers on a contact sheet before building scenes;

  Ask before writing prompts. When a gap has no photo, prefer an engraving detail, a period document or a drawn diagram.
- When AI images are used for a pre-photography era, **period wood engravings** (Flow, one master prompt) fit best beside real prints.
