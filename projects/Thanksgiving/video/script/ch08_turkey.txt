The turkey on most American tables is a breed called the Broad-Breasted White.

You may have heard it's a GMO turkey. It isn't. Nobody edited its genes in a lab. They did it the old-fashioned way: pick the biggest birds, breed them, and repeat. Generation after generation, starting back in the 1920s and '30s.

First, farmers wanted more breast meat, so they bred for a broader chest. Then in the 1950s, they swapped the turkey's dark feathers for white ones. Why? Because when you pluck a white bird, the little leftover pin feathers don't show. It looks cleaner in the store.

And the birds kept getting bigger. In the 1930s, an average turkey weighed about thirteen pounds, live. Today, it's over thirty.

[deadpan] It worked so well that the birds got too top-heavy to mate on their own. The males are too heavy. The shape is wrong. So nearly every one of them now comes from artificial insemination.

Out of the roughly 194 million turkeys raised in America last year, almost all of them are this one breed.
