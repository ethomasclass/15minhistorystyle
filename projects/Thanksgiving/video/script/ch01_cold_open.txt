The people at the first Thanksgiving knew how to catch these with their feet.

Eels. A Wampanoag man named Squanto showed the English colonists the trick. You wade out onto the mud flats, and you tread them out. "Fat and sweet," one colonist wrote. By that fall, they could fill a whole barrel with eels in a single night.

Now look at what we eat. A turkey. Green beans under fried onions. Marshmallows on sweet potatoes. And this.

By the industry's own count, Americans eat about forty-six million turkeys every Thanksgiving. And we open about seventy million cans of cranberry sauce a year, most of them in about two months.

It's a great meal. Honestly, it should be. But almost none of it was on the table in 1621.

So how did we get from eels... to *this*? And why is the cranberry sauce shaped like a can?
