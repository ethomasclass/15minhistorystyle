May 1942. A school in Connecticut. American kids salute the flag. Like *this*.

That was the salute for the Pledge of Allegiance. In schools across the country, for fifty years.

So where did it come from? Columbus Day.

In 1892, America was celebrating 400 years since Columbus arrived. A popular kids' magazine wanted schools at the center of it. One of its writers, Francis Bellamy, wrote a short pledge to the flag. And a salute: right arm, straight out toward the flag.

That October, millions of schoolchildren said it together for the very first time.

Then came the 1920s and '30s. Fascists in Italy and Nazis in Germany adopted a salute that looked almost the same. By 1942, America was at war with both.

So that December, Congress changed it. Right hand, over the heart.

So why were American kids saluting like that? Because it was the Pledge's salute, decades before the Nazis made it infamous.
