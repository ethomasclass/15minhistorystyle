So how did it end? In Britain, not with a law.

Doctors kept writing. Newspapers kept printing. And shoppers got scared. By the 1880s, "arsenic-free" was something you put in the ad.

Other countries did pass bans. Sweden in 1876. Germany in the 1880s. But Britain never banned arsenic wallpaper. It didn't have to. By about 1900, nobody wanted it, so nobody made it.

In America, Draper's "leave it alone" idea got a long test. Massachusetts banned arsenic in toys and candy in 1891. Wallpaper and fabric? 1900. That's 28 years after his report.

But the green didn't disappear. It moved. Around the same time, a beetle was eating its way across America's potato fields. Farmers found that Paris green killed it. So the same poison Americans were tearing off their walls, they were now dusting onto their potatoes. It became one of the first big insecticides in the country.

But that's a story for another time.
