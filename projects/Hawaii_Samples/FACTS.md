# Fact sheet: Hawaiʻi technique samples

Every date, number, quotation and caption on screen, with its source. Checked 22 November 2026.

## Sources

| Key | Source |
|---|---|
| **PL** | Public Law 103-150 (the "Apology Resolution", 23 November 1993), 107 Stat. 1510. Text from govinfo.gov, `STATUTE-107-Pg1510.pdf` (saved as `research/apology.pdf`). |
| **NARA** | U.S. National Archives, "Petition Against the Annexation of Hawaii" teaching page, archives.gov/education/lessons/hawaii-petition (excerpt saved as `research/nara_lesson_excerpt.txt`). |
| **FRUS** | *Foreign Relations of the United States, 1894*, Appendix II (Affairs in Hawaii), Stevens to Foster, 1 February 1893. Quoted from memory of the published text; **check against a scan before broadcast**. |

## Claims

| # | Sample | On screen | Source and wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | s1 | "The Hawaiian pear is now fully ripe, and this is the golden hour for the United States to pluck it." | FRUS (see the note above). |
| 2 | s1 | "Honolulu, February 1, 1893", "Hon. John W. Foster, Secretary of State", "JOHN L. STEVENS" | FRUS. Foster was Secretary of State until 23 February 1893. The letter is **set in type for this film**: only the one sentence is quoted, and the other lines are deliberately shown as unreadable rules. |
| 3 | s1 | "the U.S. Minister, two weeks after the overthrow" | PL: overthrow on 17 January 1893, and on 1 February 1893 the U.S. Minister raised the American flag and proclaimed a protectorate. |
| 4 | s1 | "Honolulu, January 1893" (over the landing-force photo) | Hawaiʻi State Archives PP-36-3-002, captioned as the USS Boston's landing force on duty in Honolulu. The exact day is not on the caption, so no day is given. |
| 5 | s2 | Jan 14: "The U.S. Minister and a small group of non-Hawaiian residents plan to overthrow it" | PL: "on January 14, 1893, John L. Stevens … conspired with a small group of non-Hawaiian residents … to overthrow …" |
| 6 | s2 | Jan 16: "Armed sailors and marines from the USS Boston come ashore in Honolulu" | PL: "caused armed naval forces of the United States to invade … on January 16, 1893". The commonly cited figure of 162 men is **not** used, because it is not in the sources checked. |
| 7 | s2 | Jan 17, afternoon: "The Committee of Safety deposes the monarchy; Sanford Dole heads it" | PL: "on the afternoon of January 17, 1893, a Committee of Safety … deposed the Hawaiian monarchy and proclaimed the establishment of a Provisional Government". NARA names Dole as president of the Provisional Government. |
| 8 | s2 | "I yield to the superior force of the United States of America … I do this under protest." (Liliʻuokalani, 17 January 1893) | Her protest as quoted in PL: "That I yield to the superior force of the United States of America …" and "I do this under protest". The ellipsis joins two sentences of the same statement. |
| 9 | s3 | 21,269 signatures, 556 pages | NARA: "signed by 21,269 native Hawaiian people"; "the 556-page petition". |
| 10 | s3 | "collected Sept. 11 – Oct. 2, 1897" | NARA: "Between September 11 and October 2, 1897". |
| 11 | s3 | "Honolulu, September 11, 1897" | The page itself: "Sept 11 1897", District of Honolulu, Kona, Island of Oʻahu. |
| 12 | s3 | "kūʻē: to oppose, to resist" | Hawaiian dictionary sense of kūʻē (Pukui & Elbert): to oppose, resist; opposition. |
| 13 | s3 | The stack of pages behind the petition | **Illustrative**: blank sheets standing in for the 556 pages. They show no writing. |
| 14 | s4 | "Liliʻuokalani · Queen of Hawaiʻi, 1891–1893" | Her protest of 19 December 1898 (`nara_hawaiiqueen.jpg`): "proclaimed Queen of the Hawaiian Islands on the 29th day of January, 1891". PL: the overthrow on 17 January 1893. |
| 15 | s4 | The palace, its flag and the room | **Illustration**. The Hawaiian flag is drawn with 8 stripes and the Union in the canton. |
| 16 | s5 | "August 12, 1898 · the Hawaiian flag comes down" | The LoC print's caption reads "August 12th, 1898" (`loc_annexation_transfer`). A second LoC caption says Aug. 13; the 12th is the Honolulu date and the one generally given. PL: the Newlands Resolution was signed 7 July 1898. |
| 17 | s5 | The U.S. flag in the illustration | Drawn with 45 stars (the flag of 1896–1908), simplified to 30 dots at this size. |
