How did betting go from part of baseball, to the game's unforgivable sin, and back again? In 1921, Judge Kenesaw Mountain Landis banned eight Chicago White Sox players for life, the day after a jury found them not guilty. In 2026, Major League Baseball named an official prediction market partner. But before 1919, gambling in baseball wasn't taboo. It was everywhere.

This is the history of gambling in baseball before the Black Sox: pool selling at 1860s ballgrounds, the first fixed game (the 1865 New York Mutuals), William Hulbert's National League and the 1877 Louisville Grays scandal, umpire Dick Higham, the 1908 attempt to bribe umpire Bill Klem, Hal Chase, and then the 1919 World Series fix: Comiskey (myth vs. records), Chick Gandil, Arnold Rothstein, Eddie Cicotte's signal pitch, Shoeless Joe Jackson's .375, the grand jury, "Say it ain't so, Joe" (a legend Jackson denied), the 1921 trial and Landis's lifetime bans. We finish with Rule 21, Pete Rose, the 2018 Supreme Court ruling on sports betting, the 2025 reinstatements, and baseball's 2026 prediction-market deal, with what supporters and critics each see in it.
