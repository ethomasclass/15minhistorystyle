Baseball and betting grew up together.

In the 1860s, baseball is America's hot new game, and crowds bet on it the same way they bet on horse races. Gamblers run something called {pool selling}: they auction off shares in a betting pool on a game, often right there at the ballpark.

And if people are betting on games, someone is going to try to rig one.

September 28th, 1865. Hoboken, New Jersey. The New York Mutuals, one of the best teams around, play the Brooklyn Eckfords. The Mutuals' catcher, William Wansley, has a terrible day. Six passed balls. The Eckfords score 11 runs in one inning and win, 23 to 11.

Bad day? Not exactly. A gambler had reportedly paid Wansley 100 dollars to lose. Wansley brought in two teammates and gave them a cut. That's called {throwing a game}: losing on purpose, for money. It's the first fixed game in baseball we can document.

All three players were banned. Within five years, all three were back.

Remember that. It's going to keep happening.
