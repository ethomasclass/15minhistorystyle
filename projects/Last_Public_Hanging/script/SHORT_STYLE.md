# Standalone Shorts: presentation ideas

The goal is to keep the channel's look (a dark desk, black-and-white archival images, one coral subject, teal handwriting, orange tape titles, 12 fps steps, grain) while building for a phone held at arm's length, a viewer who decides in about a second, and a feed that rewards rewatching. Ideas marked ★ are proposed for *The Last Public Execution*.

## 1. ★ One photo, marked up
Most of the Short stays on **one big archival photo** (here, the crowd) and the story is drawn onto it in teal: a circle around the scaffold, an arrow to the rooftops, a tally ("~20,000"), labels. The camera pushes in on each detail as it's named.
- **Why it works on a phone:** one image fills the screen, so nothing shrinks to a postage stamp. Marking up the photo itself is the "documents marked up in their own pixels" move from *Grip Tighter*, at full size.
- **Kit:** `Parallax` (frame 1080×1920) plus `Note` / `Trace` placed over the photo.

## 2. ★ Case file
A manila folder sits on the desk. Each beat pulls one document out and slaps it down full width: the indictment line, the jury time, the sheriff's portrait, the front page. Each gets a stamp (CHARGED, 4½ MIN, 1938).
- **Why:** the "next document" gives a reason to keep watching every 8–10 seconds and a sound to cut on. It's the *Black Sox* BANNED ledger in vertical form.
- **Kit:** the `Desk` card, the stamp sfx, `page_turn.wav`.

## 3. ★ Persistent topic strip
A thin orange tape title stays at the top the whole time (y 160–230): **THE LAST PUBLIC EXECUTION · 1936**. Viewers who join mid-scroll know what they're watching without waiting for the hook to repeat.
- **Kit:** the torn-tape title, shrunk. It sits under the logo, clear of the search icon.

## 4. ★ A ticking number
One number changes on screen as the story moves: a crowd counter climbing to 20,000 in the hook, then a stopwatch for the jury that stops at 4:30.
- **Why:** numbers that move get read and get remembered.
- **Kit:** the chart counters, in Nanum Pen teal.

## 5. ★ Loop ending
The last frame before the end card matches the first frame (the crowd photo, same crop). The closing line ("the end of watching") lands on the image the Short opened on, so a replay feels seamless and replays count.

## 6. Vertical map drop
A tall strip of the state map (or the 1910 Sanborn sheet of Owensboro) scrolls down to a pin. It's a phone-native version of the 1836 map establishing shot. Good for Shorts whose hook is a place.

## 7. Then/now split (top/bottom)
The 9:16 frame splits into two 9:8 halves: the period document on top, its modern counterpart below. This is the *Black Sox* "BANNED / PARTNER" idea as a layout. **Not for this Short**, since we're keeping it apolitical and free of current events.

## 8. Three-beat counter
A small teal "1 / 3, 2 / 3, 3 / 3" in the corner for list-shaped Shorts ("3 times baseball tried to ban gambling"). It promises a short, finite watch.

## Rules that carry over unchanged
- Captions: Inter 800, three words at a time, the spoken word in orange, at y ~1190 on a soft band.
- Safe text area: x 60–930, y 150–1150. Source tags at y ~1490.
- The first frame carries the hook visually (photo plus the key number) before a word is spoken.
- Like/subscribe end card: arrows point at YouTube's own buttons, never sitting under them (see SCRIPT.md, *End card layout*).
