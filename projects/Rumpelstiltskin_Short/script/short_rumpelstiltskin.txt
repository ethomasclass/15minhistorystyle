You've probably seen this. A tiny man in a sparkly tuxedo tiptoes through a barn at night. It's labeled "Rumpelstiltskin, 1987." And a lot of people say they *remember* it.

They don't. It was made with AI, in 2026.

Here's why it feels familiar. There really was a Rumpelstiltskin in 1987. A musical from Cannon Films, with Billy Barty in the title role. The barn scene just isn't in it.

That's how memory works. Your brain doesn't play back a recording. It *rebuilds* the scene, and fills the gaps with whatever fits. A real title. A real actor. The right decade. And the fake slides right in.

The story itself is much older. The Brothers Grimm published it in 1812. A miller brags that his daughter can spin straw into gold. A little man does the spinning, for her necklace, her ring, and then her firstborn child. She gets out of it only by guessing his name.

In 1812, he storms off. By 1857, he tears himself in two. Okay. Dramatic.

Some researchers think versions of it are thousands of years old. People have been retelling it ever since. This year, a computer did too.
