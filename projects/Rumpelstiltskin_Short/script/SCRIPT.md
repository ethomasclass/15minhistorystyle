## Fact-check flags

1. **The clip is AI, and labeled "1987."** It spread from about August 2026 and was posted on X labeled "Rumpelstiltskin (1987 film)." The creators and the tool aren't publicly known, so the script says only "made with AI, in 2026." The dates come mostly from AI-video-tool sites, so re-check before posting.
2. **"A lot of people say they remember it."** This is the widely shared claim in posts about the clip. It's phrased as what people say, not as a measured count.
3. **The 1987 film.** *Rumpelstiltskin*, the first of the Cannon Movie Tales, released in the US in April 1987 (AFI: April 10, 1987). Written and directed by David Irving, with Amy Irving as Katie, the miller's daughter, and Billy Barty as Rumpelstiltskin (his only lead role). It's a musical, which matches "A musical from Cannon Films."
4. **"The barn scene just isn't in it."** The AI clip isn't from the film. If the film has a scene set in hay or a barn (the straw-spinning is in a room of straw), the line still holds: the *viral clip* isn't in it.
5. **Memory rebuilds, it doesn't replay.** This is mainstream memory research (reconstructive memory, false memory). The script names no researcher, per the channel's rule.
6. **1812.** "Rumpelstilzchen" appeared in the first edition of the Grimms' *Kinder- und Hausmärchen*, 1812.
7. **The bargain.** In the Grimm version she gives a necklace, then a ring, then the promise of her firstborn child. She saves the child by guessing his name.
8. **Two endings.** In 1812 he runs off angrily and never comes back. In the 1857 edition he stamps his right foot into the ground and tears himself in two.
9. **"Thousands of years old."** A 2016 study of folktale lineages by researchers at Durham University and NOVA University Lisbon estimated the tale type at roughly 4,000 years old (published in Royal Society Open Science; confirm the citation before posting). That's an estimate from statistical modeling, so the script says "some researchers think" and "thousands," not a number.
10. **The name.** It's usually read as "little rattle-stilt," a goblin said to rattle posts. It isn't in the narration, but it's a good on-screen note if there's room.
