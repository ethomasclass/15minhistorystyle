```
How did a paper balloon from Japan end up in an Oregon forest in 1945, and why did nobody warn the kids who found it? In WWII, Japan launched about 9,300 balloon bombs across the Pacific on the jet stream, and the U.S. government kept every one of them out of the news.

This is the story of Japan's Fu-Go balloon bombs explained in 15 minutes: the Doolittle Raid and the revenge plan, the schoolgirls who glued the paper balloons in Tokyo's theaters, the teenage boys who launched them from Japan's beaches, the balloons landing across North America, the geologists who traced their sand back to Japan, the press blackout run by Byron Price's Office of Censorship, and the picnic at Gearhart Mountain near Bly, Oregon, where six people died. Along the way we ask whether the silence was the right call: it kept Japan blind and helped end the attacks, and it left six people without a warning.
```
