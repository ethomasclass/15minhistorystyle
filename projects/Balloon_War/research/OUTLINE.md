# The Balloon War: outline (draft 2, built on story loops)

Working title: **The Balloon War: Fake Tanks, Paper Bombs, and D-Day**
Alt: *WWII Was Fought With Balloons (Really)*
Target: about 10 minutes, about 1,800 words, 7 chapters. Written to "Write for someone who knows nothing" and
"Story loops" (skill: references/script-writing.md).

Research: `01_ghost_army.md`, `02_balloon_bombs.md`, `03_barrage_balloons_320th.md` (every claim sourced and tagged).

## Driving question (the outer loop)
**How do you fight a war with air?** Three balloons, three jobs: one protected, one lied, one killed.

## The long loop (planted in the cold open, closed in chapter 6)
"One of them crossed an ocean and killed six people in Oregon. And almost nobody was told." (True: the press
blackout. Never say "cover-up".) It stays open from about 0:40 to about 7:30 and carries the middle.

## Chapters (each ends by opening the next loop)

### 1 · Look up (cold open, ~0:50)
- **Open on now:** February 2023. Americans looked up and saw a white balloon the size of three buses drifting across
  the country. For a week, everybody was looking up. *(Facts only, no politicians.)*
- **Turn:** eighty years earlier, the sky was full of them. And in World War Two, balloons had three jobs.
  One protected soldiers. One lied to an entire army. **(A)**
- **The long loop:** "And one crossed an ocean and killed six people in Oregon. And almost nobody was told." **(B)**
- **Question out loud:** "So how do you fight a war with air?"
- **Last line (opens C):** "It starts on a beach in France, with a balloon on a leash."

### 2 · The protector (~2:00)
- **C:** what a barrage balloon is, plainly: a balloon on a steel cable. **But** a diving plane can hit the cable,
  **so** enemy pilots stay high, where it's hard to aim. **Closes C.**
- D-Day (the one date): small balloons, about 35 feet long, walked onto the beaches by hand.
- **Opens D:** "And the men holding the cables weren't who most people picture when they think of D-Day."
- **Closes D:** the 320th, about 600 Black soldiers, the only all-Black combat unit on the beaches (exact wording;
  about 2,000 Black soldiers landed that day). **But** at home, they'd watched German prisoners of war served in
  restaurants that wouldn't serve them. Fighting for a country that segregated you. (Double V, one sentence.)
- **Opens E:** "One of them, a medic, would wait eighty years for his medal."
- **Closes E:** Waverly Woodson. Wounded before he reached the sand, he treated about 200 men over about 30 hours.
  His records were lost. In 2024 a general laid the Distinguished Service Cross in the sand at Omaha Beach.
  *(Today bridge. Re-check Medal of Honor status before voicing.)*
- **Last line (opens F):** "Those balloons protected an army. A few miles inland, another unit was using balloons to
  invent one."

### 3 · The fake (~2:00, the funny chapter)
- **Closes F:** "The US Army had a unit whose weapons were inflatable tanks." 93 pounds. About 1,100 men, many of them
  artists: rubber tanks, speakers blasting recorded tank engines, fake radio chatter, fake patches, gossip in cafes.
- **Opens G:** "But could a rubber tank actually fool anyone?"
- Build: a few French cyclists saw four soldiers pick up a tank (one veteran's memory: "The Americans are very
  strong."). 21 big fake operations. Fake military police "closed" bars for a division that wasn't coming.
- **Closes G:** a colonel who walked right up to one: "Well, son, you certainly could have fooled me."
- **Opens H:** "So why have you probably never heard of them?" **Closes H:** it stayed secret for more than forty
  years. Their Congressional Gold Medal came in 2024. *(Today bridge: inflatable decoys are still made for today's
  wars; a maker wants the enemy to waste a missile worth four times more.)*
- Mid-video subscribe (wry, short).
- **Last line (opens I):** "On the other side of the world, Japan had balloons too. Theirs weren't fake."

### 4 · The paper balloon (~1:30)
- **Closes I:** paper balloons about 33 feet wide, glued from small sheets with paste made from a root vegetable.
  Made mostly by schoolgirls who were never told what for, in theaters and a sumo hall. Hungry workers ate the paste.
- **Opens J:** "But how do you get a paper balloon across the biggest ocean on Earth?"
- **Closes J:** a high, fast river of wind. Sandbags drop when it sinks, so it bobs across in about three days, then
  drops its bombs. *(Today bridge: it's the same wind that makes your flight east faster than your flight west.)*
- About 9,300 launched. About 300 found, from Alaska to Mexico to Michigan.
- **Last line (opens K):** "Balloons were landing all over North America. So why didn't anyone hear about it?"

### 5 · Silence (~1:00)
- **Closes K:** newspapers said nothing. The government asked them not to, **so** Japan wouldn't know they worked.
- **But/therefore:** it worked. Japan heard nothing, **so** it gave up in April 1945.
- One shut down power at Hanford, where the US was making material for the atomic bomb (one clause).
- **Closes the "why" half of B:** "But silence has a cost. Nobody warned the people who lived where the balloons came
  down." *(Today bridge: your phone gets an emergency alert in seconds; in 1945 the choice was to say nothing.)*
- **Last line:** "Which brings us to a Sunday in Oregon."

### 6 · Bly, Oregon (~1:15, *heavy: no jokes, slower voice*)
- **Closes B:** May 5, 1945. A pastor's wife, Elsie Mitchell, pregnant, and five children on a Sunday-school picnic in
  the woods. They found something in the trees. It exploded. All six died. Her husband survived.
- The only people killed by enemy action on the US mainland in the whole war. Afterward, the government warned the public.
- *(Today bridge: they're still out there. One was found in British Columbia in 2014.)*
- **Last line (opens L):** "Forty years later, a package arrived in Bly."

### 7 · The answer and the cranes (~1:20)
- **Closes L:** 1987. A thousand paper cranes, folded by the women who'd made those balloons as schoolgirls, carried to
  Bly by a Japanese American man who as a boy had been held in a camp about fifty miles away. (Say "regret", never
  "apology".)
- **Closes A:** "So how do you fight a war with air? You hold it on a cable to protect men your country wouldn't treat
  as equals. You pump it into rubber and lie with it. Or you fill paper with it, and let the wind decide."
- **Closing line (candidates):** "A war that started with paper balloons ended, for one small town, with paper birds."
  / "Same paper. Different message."
- End: standard subscribe line after the closing line.

## Handle with care (from the research)
- **Don't say:** "declassified in 1996"; "saved 15–30,000 lives"; "sand detectives stopped the attacks"; "germ
  balloons"; "the only Black soldiers at D-Day"; "Dabney, the last survivor"; "cover-up".
- **Tell as memory:** the four-men-lift-a-tank story.
- **Conflicting numbers:** balloon height at Normandy (200 ft vs under 2,000 ft): avoid a number; Bly ages: say
  "five children"; how long the 320th stayed in France: avoid a number.
- **Re-check right before voicing:** Woodson's Medal of Honor status; the 320th Congressional Gold Medal bill.
- **The 2023 balloon:** what, when, where it was shot down. No politicians, no blame (objectivity rules).
