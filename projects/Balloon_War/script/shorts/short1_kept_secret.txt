Japanese bombs fell on America in 1945. [serious] And the government kept it secret.

The bombs came on giant paper balloons, riding the wind five thousand miles across the Pacific. Hundreds came down. Oregon. Montana. Wyoming. Even Alaska.

So the government asked every newspaper and radio station in the country: don't report them. Not one word. If Japan never heard the balloons were working, it would stop sending them. And it did.

But nobody warned the public, either. On May 5, 1945, a pastor's wife and five kids on a church picnic in Oregon found one in the woods. It exploded. All six were killed, the only people killed by enemy action on the American mainland in the whole war.

[serious] The silence worked. And it cost six lives.
