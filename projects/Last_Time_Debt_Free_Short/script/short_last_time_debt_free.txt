February 1837. New York City. Thousands of hungry people storm a flour warehouse and roll the barrels into the street.

Just two years earlier, the national debt was *zero*. The only time in U.S. history. Today, it's more than *$40 trillion*.

So what went wrong?

President Andrew Jackson hated debt. Land sales were booming, and he paid off every cent. Washington threw a party.

But that boom was a bubble. People borrowed to buy land they'd never seen, and banks printed paper money to keep up. Then the government said: pay for land in gold or silver. Money got tight, and food got expensive.

By spring, banks ran short. People lined up to pull out their savings, and hundreds of banks failed. The Panic of 1837 turned into a depression that lasted about six years. Historians still argue over exactly why.

So, the last time America was debt-free? 1835. It lasted about two years.
