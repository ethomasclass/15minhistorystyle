# The American West after the Civil War: a four-part series

**15 Minute History** · series plan (phase 2, step 1, for four videos) · each episode targets **about 15:00 finished**

**Unit question** (every episode answers one piece of it out loud):
> To what extent did the United States fulfill its promises of freedom and opportunity after the Civil War?

**Series closer** (Ep 4 asks it and stops, Harkness-style):
> Did Manifest Destiny fulfill its promise, and for whom?

## The story arc every episode follows

These are the channel's methods (`script-writing.md`, `explainer-craft.md`, the hook-first rules in `videos/README.md`), applied to
each episode the same way so the four feel like one series.

| Beat | What it does | Rule it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Look again** (0:00–0:20) | Open on a real artifact, already on screen at frame 0 and in the thumbnail. Describe what anyone sees, then turn on one detail. The first sentence is the hook, never a date. | Hook-first cold opens; explainer rule 1 |
| **2. Question + bigger promise** (by ~0:40) | A one-breath driving question about the thing, then a promise about what it reveals. One or two "we'll come back to…" promises. | Rule 2; cold-open promises |
| **3. Start in the action** (ch 2) | No backstory before the question. Background enters just in time ("That's where … comes in") as a short flashback. | Rule 4; ch 2 starts in the action |
| **4. One example carries each chapter** | Concrete person or object → the general point → why → result → back to the person. | Rule 6 |
| **5. Stack, then "That means"** | Before each payoff, a one-sentence recap. | Rule 7 |
| **6. Myth beat** | Each episode has one myth named and corrected kindly. | Rule 11 |
| **7. Both readings** | Supporters and critics each get their evidence; no verdict. | Objectivity rules |
| **8. Plug at about two-thirds** | After the big build, never in a heavy chapter. | Standing preference (from *The Exact Spot*) |
| **9. The quiet chapter** | One heavy chapter per episode: slower voice, no jokes, teal-only quiet mode, a primary source and space. | Heavy material rule |
| **10. "So let's go back to the question."** | Answer the driving question out loud through callbacks, fill in this episode's row of the **Promise Ledger**, then end on a short reversal **over the opening image**. | Last-chapter rule; rule 13 |
| **11. The handoff** | One line that tees up the next episode, then the end-screen thank-you. | *Fix Everything*'s tease ending |

**The series arc on top of that:** Ep 1 sets out the frame (the **Greater Reconstruction**: the question of what freedom means,
asked in the West with more people in the room). Eps 2 and 3 test the promises made to settlers. Ep 4 turns the camera around and
tests the promises made to the people already there, then asks the series question.

## Recurring devices

- **The Promise Ledger** (the series' visual anchor). A dark ledger card (`DarkPaper`, teal ruled lines, coral and orange accents; no cream or white) with three columns: *The promise · Who got it · Who
  didn't.* Same place and size every time. Each episode's last chapter writes one row; Ep 4 shows all four and stamps it. It doubles
  as the students' graphic organizer for the unit question (put a blank copy on each viewing guide).
- **One drawn 3D map that fills in** (our tilted-slab map style): Ep 1 the rail lines; Ep 2 the cattle trails and railheads; Ep 3
  homestead country, the 100th meridian and the 20-inch rain line; Ep 4 the 1851 and 1868 treaty lands shrinking to reservations,
  then allotments. Same base map and camera clamp in all four.
- **The resistance card** (Ep 4, and once as a preview in Ep 2): same layout every time (*Who · Where · When · Why · How it ended*),
  built for the matching questions.
- **Hinge years** (a dated `Stamp` each time):
  - **1862:** Homestead Act, Morrill Act and Pacific Railway Act, passed while the Southern states were out of Congress. Also the
    Dakota War. (Ep 1 introduces it; Eps 3 and 4 call back.)
  - **1868:** the Fort Laramie Treaty and the Diné treaty that sent the Navajo home. Grant is elected. (Eps 1, 2, 4.)
  - **1874:** Black Hills gold, the barbed-wire patent, the grasshopper summer, Turkey Red wheat, the Red River War. (Eps 2, 3, 4.)
  - **1890:** the census says the frontier line is gone; Wounded Knee. (Eps 3, 4.)
- **160 acres:** the Homestead Act's number (Ep 3) returns as the Dawes Act allotment (Ep 4).

## Series-wide decisions so far

- **Order:** Railroad → Ranching & Mining → Farming → Plains Nations (agreed).
- **Images:** archival first. This era is well photographed (A. J. Russell, Solomon Butcher, the bison-skull pile, Red Horse's
  drawings, Carlisle). AI paintings only for real gaps, if at all.
- **Names:** no historian names in narration ("historians call this…" is fine). Frederick Jackson Turner is an open question (Ep 3).
