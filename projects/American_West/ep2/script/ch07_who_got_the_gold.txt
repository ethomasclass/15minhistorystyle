Every gold rush goes the same way, in three steps.

Step one: a man with a pan in a creek. The gold is loose in the gravel, and anyone can wash it out. That's called {placer mining}.

Step two: the easy gold runs out. The rest is locked inside solid rock, deep underground.

Step three: getting it out takes shafts, machines, and a mill that crushes rock day and night. And that takes money. A lot of it.

In 1877, a group of rich California investors, led by George Hearst, bought a claim in the Black Hills called the {Homestake}, for seventy thousand dollars. [impressed] It became one of the richest gold mines in the world. Over forty million ounces of gold. It didn't close until 2002.

So, stack it up. A free-for-all, then hard rock, then a company. That means most miners ended up where they'd started. Working for wages. Just underground.
