# Forever Free: archival shot list

Save to `public/img/chNN/` with descriptive names. Get an alternate for each must-have.

## ch01 Cold Open
- MUST: Robert Smalls portrait (photo, 1860s or as congressman)
- MUST: Harper's Weekly June 14, 1862 engraving of the steamer *Planter* (and its page with Smalls's portrait)
- Charleston Harbor map or view, 1862 (Fort Sumter in the harbor)
- Union blockading fleet off Charleston (engraving)

## ch02 Contraband
- MUST: Fort Monroe (Fortress Monroe) engraving or photo, 1861
- MUST: "Stampede of slaves to Fortress Monroe" (Harper's Weekly 1861) or "Contrabands coming into camp" (Harper's)
- General Benjamin Butler portrait
- MUST: A contraband camp photo (e.g., contrabands at Foller's house, Cumberland Landing, 1862; or a freedmen's village)
- Enslaved people building Confederate fortifications (engraving)

## ch03 Lincoln's Problems
- MUST: George McClellan portrait (Brady)
- Lincoln with McClellan at Antietam (Gardner photo, October 1862) — save for ch04 too
- Peninsula Campaign: Yorktown or Seven Days engraving
- MUST: Baltimore riot of April 19, 1861 (Massachusetts troops attacked, Harper's engraving)
- Burned railroad bridge (Gunpowder River or similar)
- Roger B. Taney portrait
- Ex parte Merryman document or Fort McHenry

## ch04 Waiting for a Victory
- MUST: Horace Greeley portrait
- MUST: New York Tribune "The Prayer of Twenty Millions" page, or Lincoln's reply as printed (Aug 1862)
- First Reading of the Emancipation Proclamation (Carpenter painting engraving by A.H. Ritchie, 1866)
- MUST: Antietam: Gardner's photos of the dead are graphic; prefer Burnside Bridge, Dunker Church, or the battlefield landscape. NO close-up bodies.
- Antietam battle lithograph (Kurz & Allison is later, 1878; acceptable) or Forbes/Waud drawing
- MUST: Clara Barton portrait
- Dorothea Dix portrait
- MUST: Catholic sisters as nurses (Sisters of Charity / Daughters of Charity at a hospital; Satterlee Hospital)
- A women's work image: women in a field or Sanitary Commission

## ch05 Forever Free
- MUST: Emancipation Proclamation (the document, National Archives scan; or 1863/1864 printed versions at LoC)
- Watch Night / "Reading the Emancipation Proclamation" engravings (e.g., Harper's 1863 "Emancipation" by Thomas Nast is fine as an image of the idea)
- Union army in the South with freed people following (engraving)
- MUST: A map showing areas affected by the Proclamation (LoC has period maps); otherwise we draw it on the 3D map

## ch06 King Cotton Fails
- Lancashire cotton mill or the "cotton famine" in Britain (Illustrated London News)
- Cotton bales / cotton in Egypt or India (period engraving)
- British Parliament or Lord Palmerston portrait
- A British cartoon about the American war (Punch 1862–63) — use carefully

## ch07 The 54th
- MUST: Assault on Fort Wagner, July 18, 1863 (Kurz & Allison 1890 lithograph; or a period engraving)
- MUST: Robert Gould Shaw portrait; a 54th soldier's portrait (LoC Gladstone Collection has 54th soldiers)
- MUST: "Men of Color, To Arms!" broadside (Douglass, 1863)
- Frederick Douglass portrait (1860s)
- Lewis Douglass in uniform (photo exists)
- U.S. Colored Troops recruiting poster ("Come and Join Us Brothers," 1863–64)
- Black soldiers photo (e.g., Company E, 4th USCT, Fort Lincoln 1864)
- Robert Smalls later (as congressman)

## ch08 Who Freed Them?
- Reuse: Smalls, the Proclamation, contraband camp, Greeley letter. Optional: "Freedom to the slave" print.
