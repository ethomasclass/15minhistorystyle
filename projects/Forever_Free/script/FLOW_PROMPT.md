# Google Flow prompts: the Civil War series (wood-engraving style)

The Civil War has thousands of real photographs, engravings and documents, so AI images fill only the moments nobody recorded.
Same approach as *The Exact Spot*: a period wood engraving, one style block kept word for word, so the AI images sit beside the
real *Harper's Weekly* and *Frank Leslie's* engravings and never pass for photographs. Real documents and portraits stay as cards on
the desk.

**15 images total: 5 per video.** Must-haves are marked ★; each other scene has a fallback (a map beat, a document or a drawn diagram).

**How to run them.** Paste one video's master prompt at a time. If Flow merges scenes or makes fewer images, use the
one-scene fallback at the bottom. Test first with V1-4 (Bull Run spectators), V2-1 (the *Planter* at night) and V3-4 (Galveston). Every scene here was checked
against the archival search: each one covers a moment with no usable period image (no picture of the spectators, the
*Planter*'s escape, the three men rowing to Fort Monroe, the rations at Appomattox or the Galveston reading survives, and the
only period image of the Richmond bread riot is a racist caricature). If the engraving turns to grey mush, add "bold, clean line work, high contrast, like a woodcut" to the scene.

**Naming.** Save each as its scene name + `.png` (e.g. `v1_ch07_spectators.png`) and drop them in the chat or in the video's
`public/img/gen/` folder. I'll check every one at full size for stray text, modern details and anything that reads wrong.

## Video 1: In Their Own Words (master prompt)

```
You are illustrating a history documentary about secession and the start of the American Civil War (1860 to 1861). Create 5 separate images, one per numbered scene below. Make each one its own image in 16:9 landscape format. Do not combine scenes, do not make a grid or collage, and do not number or caption the images.

STYLE FOR EVERY IMAGE (identical across all images so they look like one series):
A black-and-white wood engraving from an American illustrated newspaper of the 1860s, such as Harper's Weekly or Frank Leslie's Illustrated Newspaper. All shading made with fine parallel lines and cross-hatching. Crisp black ink on slightly yellowed paper, white highlights cut out of the block, slight ink spread and uneven printing. Bold, clean line work and high contrast. No color, no grey gradients, no airbrushing, no painterly texture, no photographic look. Figures drawn the way period engravers drew them: slightly stiff, clear silhouettes, simple uncluttered backgrounds. Light and mood are shown only through the density of lines (dense hatching for night and shadow, open paper for daylight).

RULES FOR EVERY IMAGE:
- Every detail historically accurate for the stated year and place: clothing, hairstyles, uniforms, weapons, ships, buildings, furniture, lamps and tools. Nothing modern.
- No text of any kind: no letters, words, captions, signatures, dates, numbers, labels or watermarks anywhere, including on flags, banners, papers, signs and maps.
- No frame, border or margin. Fill the whole 16:9 image.
- No recognizable real historical person. Where a scene involves a real person, show them from behind, at a distance or in shadow, face not visible. Faces that are visible belong to ordinary anonymous people.
- One clear main subject per image, easy to separate from the background.
- No gore, no blood, no dead bodies.
- Enslaved and free Black people are drawn with dignity as individuals, never caricatured.

THE 5 SCENES:

1. v1_ch01_savannah_speech: Night, March 21, 1861, inside a crowded theater hall in Savannah, Georgia, lit by gas lamps. Seen from the back of the hall: rows of men in frock coats and a few women in hoop skirts and bonnets, all facing a stage. On the stage, small and far away, a very thin, slight man in a dark suit stands at a lectern with one arm raised, his face too distant to see. Bunting hangs from the balcony rail (no stars, no lettering). Warm, crowded, excited.

2. v1_ch04_who_voted: A cold, grey morning in early January 1861, in front of a small brick county courthouse in rural Georgia. A line of white men in frock coats, slouch hats and work clothes waits on the courthouse steps to hand paper ballots to two officials at a table on the porch. Off to one side, behind a rail fence, a few women in shawls and bonnets watch. In the far background, across a bare winter cotton field, a group of enslaved Black men and women carry firewood, not part of the scene at the courthouse. Quiet, ordinary, a little uneasy.

3. v1_ch05_last_rations: Early April 1861, inside Fort Sumter in Charleston Harbor. A brick casemate with an arched ceiling and a cannon at a gun port, its muzzle facing out to sea. Four U.S. Army soldiers in fatigue jackets and forage caps sit on ammunition boxes sharing a small amount of hard bread and a tin cup of coffee; a nearly empty barrel stands beside them. Through the gun port, far across the water, the low shoreline and faint smoke of shore batteries. Tired, thin, patient. Dim light from the gun port.

4. ★ v1_ch07_spectators: Midday, July 21, 1861, a grassy hilltop near Centreville, Virginia. In the foreground, civilians who have driven out from Washington to watch a battle: men in top hats and frock coats, a few in light summer suits, two women in bonnets with parasols, standing beside open carriages and a buggy; one man looks through opera glasses; a wicker basket sits open on the grass. Far below and miles away, across rolling farmland and woods, small puffs of white cannon smoke rise along a creek. Bright, hot, cheerful, about to go wrong.

5. ★ v1_ch07_retreat: Late afternoon, July 21, 1861, a narrow dirt road and a small stone bridge in northern Virginia. A panicked jam: Union soldiers in blue sack coats and forage caps running, a supply wagon tipped at the roadside, an army ambulance wagon, and a civilian's open carriage with a top-hatted driver whipping his horses, all crowding onto the bridge at once. Dust everywhere, a dropped picnic basket in the road. No wounds, no bodies. Chaotic and a little absurd.
```

5. v2_ch04_barton_antietam: Afternoon, September 17, 1862, a farm field near Antietam Creek, Maryland, behind the battle line. A woman in a plain dark dress with a white apron and a bonnet, seen from behind, walks beside a mule-drawn supply wagon loaded with crates and bandages toward a farmhouse being used as a field hospital, where soldiers in blue rest against the fence. Battle smoke drifting across distant cornfields. No wounds or blood. Steady and brave.

THE 5 SCENES:

1. ★ v3_ch04_bread_riot: Morning, April 2, 1863, a street of brick shops in Richmond, Virginia. A crowd of thin, determined working women in plain dresses, shawls and bonnets, some carrying empty baskets, one holding a hatchet at her side, push toward the door of a closed provisions store whose shutters are half pulled down. A few men in work clothes and boys follow behind. Shopkeepers watch from an upstairs window. Grey spring light, tense, angry and hungry, no one hurt.

2. ★ v3_ch05_georgia_farm: November 1864, a small farmhouse in middle Georgia. On the porch, a farm woman in a plain dress and shawl, an old man and two children stand looking toward the horizon, where columns of black smoke rise from a burning railroad depot and a long line of soldiers and wagons moves along a distant road. In the yard, an empty smokehouse door hangs open. Grey autumn light, bare trees. Quiet, worried, nothing violent shown.

3. ★ v3_ch06_rations: April 1865, a muddy field near Appomattox Court House, Virginia, the day after the surrender. Union soldiers in blue sack coats unload hardtack crates and sides of bacon from army wagons and hand them to a line of thin Confederate soldiers in worn grey and butternut jackets, many barefoot or in broken shoes. One Union soldier hands a tin cup to a Confederate who takes it without looking up. Spring trees just budding. Exhausted, quiet, not triumphant.

4. ★ v3_ch06_galveston: June 19, 1865, a street in Galveston, Texas. From the balcony of a two-story brick building with an iron railing, a Union officer in a frock coat reads aloud from a single sheet of paper (no readable writing). Below in the street, a crowd of Black men, women and children in work clothes and their best Sunday clothes listen; some hold each other, one older woman raises her hands. A few Union soldiers stand at the edge of the crowd. Hot, bright Gulf Coast light, palms and a ship's masts in the distance. Joy that is still being understood.

5. v3_ch07_hired_out: A winter morning, 1866, the steps of a county courthouse in rural Mississippi. A young Black man in plain work clothes stands with his hat in his hand before a seated white justice of the peace at a table on the courthouse porch; beside the table, a white planter in a long coat counts coins onto the table. A deputy stands behind the young man. Plain, unemotional, matter-of-fact; the injustice is in the arrangement, not in any violence. (Fallback: the drawn "no job → arrested → fined → hired out" diagram.)
