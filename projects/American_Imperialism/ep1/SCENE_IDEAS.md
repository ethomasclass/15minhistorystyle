# Ep 1 scene ideas: new things to try (draft for Ellis to pick from)

Everything here is new to the channel (checked against `techniques/README.md`). Each idea is tied to the line it serves.
**Recommended** marks the strongest; the rest are options. The humor rules still hold: the narrator stays deadpan, the cut
carries the joke, nothing funny in chapters 7 and 8, nothing on top of a quote.

## 1. A device for the series question

**The Scale** (recommended). A brass balance scale sits on the desk, its two pans labelled in small engraved type:
EXPRESSION and BETRAYAL. At a handful of beats a small labelled weight drops onto one pan with a dull thud, deadpan:
- *Expression:* "markets", "a strong navy", "Christianity", "a republic", "the Fourth of July"
- *Betrayal:* "the Bayonet Constitution", "Marines ashore", "no vote"

In chapter 9, on "the honest answer is both," the scale settles level. It's the series question made physical, and it can
recur in all four episodes, ending in a different position each time (Ep 4 leaves it for the viewer). Built in Blender
(brass, real weight, one camera angle that never changes), so it matches the world scenes.

## 2. Words and emphasis

- **The euphemism decoder** (recommended). A two-line card: the official name on top in fine engraved type, and what it was
  underneath in a plain typewriter face, typed on with no sound. *Committee of Safety* / armed men with rifles. *Protectorate*
  / the American flag on the government building. *Republic of Hawaii* / the same men. *Joint resolution* / a vote that
  needed fewer votes. A recurring, deadpan way of saying what the script says, which also fits the series question.
- **Letterpress stamps for the "-isms."** Isolationism, imperialism, interventionism and Social Darwinism are stamped onto the
  desk with a wooden printing block: uneven ink, slight misregistration, the block lifting away. Period pamphlet type instead
  of the orange tape (which stays for titles).
- **Scan lift.** The quoted word is cut out of the real document scan, in its own 1890s typeface, and lifted toward the
  camera with a soft shadow: Cleveland's "act of war", the petition's heading, Mahan's "coaling stations". The evidence's own
  letters do the emphasis.
- **Proofreader's marks.** On the 1887 constitution, as the narration says it "took away most of his power," red-pencil
  editor's marks strike the king's powers (the delete loop, the caret, "stet" crossed out). Period proofreading, not a modern
  highlighter.
- **The census pencil.** On the 1890 census map, a pencil and a wooden ruler search for the frontier line, hover, and lift
  away with nothing drawn: "there can hardly be said to be a frontier line."

## 3. Maps

- **The coal gauge** (recommended). A steamship crosses the tilted 3D map of the Pacific from San Francisco toward Asia,
  with a small brass coal gauge in the corner draining as it goes. It runs dry in mid-ocean. A pin drops on Hawaiʻi and the
  gauge refills. It explains Mahan's coaling stations in ten seconds with no words.
- **The string board** for the twist, chapter 5 (recommended). Four pins in four colours, one per pitcher (a factory, the
  Naval War College, a church, a lecture hall), each trailing a string. On "Four pitches. Four different reasons," the four
  strings pull taut across the Pacific to the same tack: Honolulu. Built physically in Blender, or drawn in 2D on the map.
- **Ink reaches the coast.** On the 1890 census map, settlement spreads west as ink soaking into paper, reaches the Pacific
  shore and beads up there like water on wax. On "So where next?" one drop rolls off into the ocean.
- **The pink empire.** The 1886 Imperial Federation map, with the British Empire in its period pink, stands in for "the
  biggest empire on earth." The series map then fills American holdings in a different ink, episode by episode.
- **Map-to-world dive.** The tilted map pushes in on Oʻahu, and the paper's relief rises into the Blender diorama of
  Honolulu and the palace. One move joins the map and the world (the transition style we tested with the church).
- **The sugar stream.** White sugar grains stream along a route from Honolulu to San Francisco, the stream as wide as the
  trade (about 99 percent of the crop). On the 1890 McKinley Tariff the stream stalls and grains pile up on the dock.

## 4. Numbers

- **The stack of paper** (recommended, a loop). Chapter 6 ends on "partly because of a stack of paper": show only the edge of
  a tall stack on a Senate desk, unexplained. Chapter 8 pays it off: the stack is the petition, 556 pages, and the counter
  runs to 21,269 over the names in their own handwriting. The setup costs one shot; the payoff is the episode's evidence.
- **Two cents an acre.** An 1867 two-cent coin set on a small square of the Alaska map that's one acre at the map's scale.
- **The receipt** (optional, chapter 6 only). The 1887 treaty renewal as a period cash-register receipt: sugar, duty-free;
  one harbor, Pearl. Keep it off the overthrow itself.

## What to build first

A test of three, to judge the look before committing: **the Scale** (one beat), **the coal gauge** map, and **the
euphemism decoder** card. Each is small, and between them they cover a Blender prop, a map move and a type treatment.
