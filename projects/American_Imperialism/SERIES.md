# American Imperialism, 1867-1917: a four-part series

**Chunk History** · series plan (phase 2, step 1, for four videos) · built from `HANDOFF.md` and the channel's series method
(`script-writing.md` section 10; worked example `projects/American_West/SERIES.md`)

**Unit question** (every episode answers one piece of it out loud):
> Can a republic born fighting an empire become one?

**Series closer** (Ep 4 asks it and stops, Harkness-style):
> You're the president. An island in crisis, American lives and money at stake. Do you go Roosevelt, Taft, or Wilson?
> Then, with no narration: "deriving their just powers from the consent of the governed."

| Ep | Working title | Years | The answer America gives itself | Outline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Pitch Meeting** · *How America Talked Itself Into an Empire* | 1867-1898 | "Not yet. But it's practicing." | [`EP1_PITCH_MEETING_OUTLINE.md`](EP1_PITCH_MEETING_OUTLINE.md) |
| 2 | **Whose War Was It?** · *Cuba, the Maine, and the Splendid Little War* | 1895-1898 | "We're liberators, not an empire." | to write |
| 3 | **The Road to China** · *The Philippines, the Open Door, and the War Nobody Named* | 1898-1902 | "Yes, and some Americans knew it." | to write |
| 4 | **America's Backyard** · *Big Stick, Dollars, and Morals* | 1898-1917 | "Yes, just without the name." | to write |

The order follows cause. The arguments of Ep 1 need a war to act on (Ep 2). The war leaves the U.S. holding islands in two
oceans, so the story splits: the Pacific and China (Ep 3), then the Caribbean and Latin America (Ep 4).

## The story arc every episode follows

The channel's methods, applied the same way four times, so the parts feel like one series.

| Beat | What it does |
|---|---|
| **1. Cold open from the future** (0:00-0:55) | Open on a later, dramatic moment with a real artifact on screen at frame 0 (and in the thumbnail). The first sentence is the hook, never a date. Name the danger in words. |
| **2. Question + promises** (by about 0:40) | The episode's driving question in one breath, then one or two "we'll come back to that" promises. |
| **3. Start in the action** (ch 2) | No backstory before the question. Background arrives just in time as a short flashback. |
| **4. One person carries each chapter** | Three to five real people on both sides, each with a want. Person, then the general point, then back to the person. |
| **5. Stack, then "That means"** | A one-sentence recap before each payoff. |
| **6. Myth beat** | One myth per episode, named and corrected kindly. |
| **7. Both readings** | The imperialists' own case, stated fairly, and the critics' case. No verdict from the narrator. |
| **8. The George III callback** (once, at the climax) | The Declaration on parchment. One grievance lights up. "He has" is struck through and "We have" is written over it. |
| **9. The quiet chapter** | One heavy chapter per episode: slower voice, no jokes, teal-only quiet palette, a primary source and space. This is where the ledger voice speaks. |
| **10. Plug after the climax** (about 80%) | Short and wry, while a loop is open, never in the quiet chapter. |
| **11. "So let's go back to the question."** | Answer the unit question for this episode, fill this episode's row of the Ledger, end on a short reversal over the opening image. |
| **12. The handoff** | One line that tees up the next episode, then the end-screen thank-you. |

## Recurring devices

- **The George III callback** (Ellis's favorite, the series' signature). Same parchment, same camera move, same sound every
  time, so viewers learn to expect it.

  | Ep | Grievance (exact Declaration text) | U.S. parallel |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | "For taking away our Charters, abolishing our most valuable Laws, and altering fundamentally the Forms of our Governments" | Planters force a new constitution on Hawaiʻi's king, then overthrow its queen and write their own government |
  | 2 | "He has kept among us, in times of peace, Standing Armies without the Consent of our legislatures" | U.S. troops stay in Cuba after the war |
  | 3 | "He has plundered our seas, ravaged our Coasts, burnt our towns, and destroyed the lives of our people" | The Philippine-American War (Samar, Balangiga) |
  | 4 | "He has dissolved Representative Houses repeatedly" | 1917: Marines dissolve Haiti's legislature |

  The Ep 1 grievance is verified against the National Archives transcript (`research/sources/declaration.txt`). The other three
  are to be checked the same way when each episode is written.

- **The Ledger** (the recurring card; it doubles as the students' graphic organizer). A dark ledger card with three columns:
  *What America said · What America did · Who answered back.* Same place and size every time. Each episode's last chapter writes
  one row. Ep 4 shows all four.

  | Ep | What America said | What America did | Who answered back |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | "No entangling alliances." | Took a kingdom's harbor, then the kingdom | Liliʻuokalani |
  | 2 | "We will not annex Cuba." (Teller) | Won Spain's colonies in under three months | José Martí |
  | 3 | "An open door for all." | Fought Filipinos who had been its allies; shut the door on Chinese people | Emilio Aguinaldo, Wu Tingfang |
  | 4 | "Democracy and human rights." | Policed the hemisphere | To choose (see `HANDOFF.md` section 7) |

- **One drawn map that fills in** (the channel's tilted 3D map, same base and camera clamp in all four): the Pacific and the
  Caribbean on one sheet. Ep 1 adds Alaska, Midway, Hawaiʻi and Pearl Harbor. Ep 2 adds Cuba, Puerto Rico, Guam and the
  Philippines, then splits into two oceans. Ep 3 draws the stepping stones to China. Ep 4 fills the Caribbean and cuts the canal.

- **Cold opens from the future** and **the other side of the ledger** (one real voice from the receiving end per episode), as
  set out in `HANDOFF.md` section 3.

- **Hinge years** (a dated stamp the first time, a callback after):
  - **1867:** Alaska bought, Midway claimed. The first steps off the continent.
  - **1890:** three things in one year. The census says the frontier line is gone, Mahan publishes his book, and the McKinley
    Tariff wrecks Hawaiian sugar.
  - **1893:** the overthrow in Honolulu (and a depression at home that sharpens the hunt for markets).
  - **1898:** the war with Spain, and Hawaiʻi annexed in the middle of it.
  - **1917:** Haiti's legislature dissolved. The year the U.S. enters a war "to make the world safe for democracy."

## Open decisions (from `HANDOFF.md` section 7, with a recommendation for each)

1. **Runtime.** Recommend the channel standard: about 15:00 finished, which is about 2,300 words of narration at the current pace.
2. **Visual style.** Recommend two modes that alternate: archival pictures on the gridded desk (black, with teal and coral
   surfaces for variety), and illustrated "journey" sequences that fly from scene to scene through doorways, made from Google
   Flow plates (tested on the Hawaiʻi overthrow in `projects/Hawaii_Samples/`). Two or three journeys per episode.
3. **Ep 4 ledger voice.** Decide when Ep 4 is outlined.
4. **Classroom companions.** The Ledger is built to be the graphic organizer if they are wanted.
5. **Gilded Age link.** Ep 1 can open its pitch with the overproduction problem in one line, with or without naming the earlier series.
