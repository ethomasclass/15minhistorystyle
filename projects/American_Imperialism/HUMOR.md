# Humor for the series: deadpan, mockumentary, dry

How this series is funny, based on research into *The Office*, *Parks and Recreation*, *Arrested Development* and dry-humor
history channels. It applies to all four episodes. Sources are listed at the end.

## What the research says

1. **The camera tells the truth the people won't.** In *The Office*, the interviews let characters say what they feel while
   the camera shows what they aren't saying; the people who made it described the camera as "subtext." **For us:** the
   narration and the real quotes are what people *said*; the cut is what they *did*. That is also the series' Ledger (*What
   America said · What America did*), so the jokes and the argument are the same thing.
2. **The contradiction cut** (TV writers call it a "Gilligan cut"): someone declares something, and the very next shot shows
   the opposite. It is the cleanest dry joke there is, and with real quotes it is also evidence.
3. **The look.** Jim Halpert looks into the camera whenever something unbelievable happens. He is the audience's stand-in:
   the look says "you saw that too, right?" It works because it is rare and always at the unbelievable moment.
4. **The camera is always a little late.** *The Office*'s directors framed scenes so the camera seems to be catching up to
   events, which sells the documentary. A camera that arrives just after something happened is funny on its own.
5. **The reliable, dispassionate narrator.** *Arrested Development*'s narrator was modeled on a nature documentary: calm,
   omniscient, and quietly pointing out the gap between what the characters say and what is true. He never jokes. He just
   corrects.
6. **Deadpan is a flat delivery against an absurd fact.** Understatement does the work. Timing comes from sentence length:
   a short sentence after a long one is the punchline.
7. **Rule of three and escalation.** A running gag is set up, repeated, and paid off, a little bigger each time.
8. **Accuracy.** Research on history channels finds humor keeps its credibility when it never bends the facts. Every gag here
   is true.

## The rules for this series

- **The narrator never jokes and never notices a joke.** Calm, factual, like a nature documentary. At most a short, true,
  flat sentence after a long one ("Dole refused.").
- **The joke is in the cut.** Say, then show. No music stings, no comedy sound effects: room tone, wind, or one real sound.
- **Holds of 1 to 2 seconds**, then back on the next word. Never wait for a laugh.
- **The camera acts like a documentary crew:** handheld, a slow creep, a snap zoom, a shot through a doorway, a tilt that
  arrives too late.
- **The look belongs to one character: Jefferson's portrait.** He is the series' Jim. Every time America does the opposite of
  what it said, the camera cuts to him. Same framing, same slow creep, every episode. No one else looks at the camera.
- **Captions are true and plain.** A lower third can be the joke if it is just a fact.
- **About five gags an episode,** with one running gag in threes.
- **Never aimed at** the people on the receiving end (in Ep 1: the Queen, Native Hawaiians, cane workers). Nothing from the
  George III callback to the end of the quiet chapter. Never on top of a quote from the receiving end.

## Episode 1 gag sheet

| # | Kind | The line | The cut |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contradiction | "Critics called it 'Seward's Folly.'" | On "Folly," a gold nugget drops onto the desk with a dull thud. 1 s of nothing. |
| 2 | The look, 1 of 3 | "...a lot of Americans did not want to be left out." | Hard cut to Jefferson's portrait on the pitch-room wall (he was quoted, straight, in ch 3: "entangling alliances with none"). Slow 3 percent creep, 1.5 s. |
| 3 | Contradiction | "They called themselves the Committee of Safety." | Hard cut on "Safety" to a handheld shot through a doorway: men laying rifles on a table. Snap zoom. 1.5 s. |
| 4 | True caption | "...John Stevens, ordered sailors and Marines ashore..." | Lower third, held a beat long: JOHN L. STEVENS / U.S. Minister to Hawaiʻi / Not authorized by Congress. |
| 5 | Late camera, then the look, 2 of 3 | "On February 1st, Stevens raised the American flag..." | The camera tilts up a flagpole, a second late: the flag is already up and the ceremony is breaking up. Cut to Jefferson. |
| 6 | Running gag, 3 beats | "Sanford Dole. Yes. That Dole." / "Dole refused." / "...his cousin James arrived and started growing pineapples." | A pineapple in the corner of the desk, unexplained. Then halfway across the desk; nobody moved it. Then touching Dole's portrait, and a hard cut to a pineapple-can label. |
| 7 | The look, 3 of 3 | "...then took the islands anyway." | The empty pitch room. Jefferson. 2 s. |

Cut from earlier drafts: the wagon driver looking at the camera (the look belongs to Jefferson, and the frontier closing isn't
absurd); the pitch-deck titles and the calendar page (staging, not jokes).
