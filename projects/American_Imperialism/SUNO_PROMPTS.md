# Suno prompts: American Imperialism, 1867-1898 (Episode 1 first)

Copy each **Style** box and its **Lyrics** box (structure tags only) into Suno. Everything else is labels.

**Why these tempos:** the narration runs about 185 words a minute, so the cues stay slow to mid tempo and sparse, with room
for the voice. I cut on musical phrases, so the exact BPM doesn't need to match anything. The humor cuts play in **silence**
(no music under them), so every cue needs a few clean stops or drop-outs I can cut into.

## Settings (same for every cue)

- **Custom** mode, **Instrumental OFF**, and paste only the structure tags in the Lyrics box (they start with [Instrumental],
  so there are no words to sing). If a take hums or sings, throw it out.
- **Exclude styles** (paste every time):

```
vocals, choir, lyrics, humming, whistling, drum kit, EDM, trap, pop, rock, synth lead, dubstep, rap, ukulele strumming, luau, tiki, hula, slack key cliché, steel guitar, exotica, comedic, cartoon, western movie theme, epic trailer
```

- Make **2 to 4 takes**; keep the one whose **first 30 to 40 seconds** already sound right.
- If a take is too short, **Extend** from its last good section. Longer is always fine: I trim and fade.
- **Download as MP3**, named exactly as shown (e.g. `ai_theme.mp3`), and send them back. I level them about 13 LU under the
  voice.

**On Hawaiʻi and the music:** no "island" clichés. The Hawaiian side of the story is carried by restraint (piano, cello, a
nylon guitar played simply), not by a tourist sound. One idea for later, separate from Suno: Liliʻuokalani was a composer, and
her song *Aloha ʻOe* (1878) is in the public domain. A quiet solo-piano arrangement of it, made by us, could sit under her own
words in the quiet chapter. Say if you want that and I'll prepare it; it would replace cue 6.
