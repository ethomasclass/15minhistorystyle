# Suno prompts: American Imperialism, Episode 1 (v5, documentary score, one feeling per scene)

v3 and v4 tied every cue to one melody; it got to be too much. v5 drops the motif. Each cue is ordinary documentary
scoring (strings, piano, pulses, percussion, pads), built around **what the viewer should feel at that moment**. The
variety comes from the scenes: wonder, a wink, persuasion, dread, grief, resolve.

## How the score steers the viewer

- **The music tells them how to feel; the narration tells them what happened.** Under deadpan narration, the cue
  carries the emotion, so the voice never has to.
- **Play the persuasion straight.** The pitches should sound convincing. If the music mocks them, the
  viewer stops thinking. The irony comes from the cuts and the facts.
- **Earn the dark turn.** The first half of the episode stays light and curious, so the coup lands. Nothing ominous
  before chapter 6 except the cold open.
- **Silence is a cue too.** The Declaration line is always silent (the cue before it stops dead). Humor cuts go to silence,
  and the music comes back on a new phrase. No music under "I yield to the superior force" or the Queen's own words.
- **Never louder than the voice.** I level every cue about 13 LU under the narration and reject any take that hides
  consonants.

## How to make them

- **Custom** mode. Paste the **Style** box and the **Lyrics** box (structure tags only, no words). **Instrumental** on
  (or whichever setting kept your last cues free of voices).
- **Exclude styles**, every time, plus each cue's extras:
  ```
  vocals, choir, humming, wordless vocals
  ```
- Make **2 to 4 takes**. Keep the one whose **first 30 seconds** already feel right. Too short: **Extend** from the last good
  section. Download **MP3**, named exactly as shown. I trim, fade and cut the stops myself.
- **Still banned** (the channel's overused sounds and the island clichés): felt piano, pizzicato strings, ticking clocks,
  solo cello over a pad, clarinet and bassoon, ukulele, steel or slack-key guitar, hula drums, music box. Never type
  "Hawaiian", "island" or "tropical". No artist or film names (Suno refuses them).
