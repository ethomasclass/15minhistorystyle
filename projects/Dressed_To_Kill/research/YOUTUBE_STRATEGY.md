# YouTube strategy: Arsenic Green (working title)

Research for the Halloween-season video on the Victorian arsenic-green craze (Scheele's Green / Paris Green).
Compiled 14 Oct 2026. YouTube numbers come from YouTube search-result pages scraped on 14 Oct 2026 (US, English), so they are
approximate and will drift. Google Trends numbers come from the Trends API (US, last 5 years, weekly).
Where a claim is creator folklore and not evidence, it is marked **[folklore]**. Where it comes from YouTube itself, it is marked **[YouTube]**.

---

## Top 10 actions

1. **Lead with the American angle the field has skipped.** About 40 videos tell the British wallpaper/dress story. Almost none follow it
   to the U.S.: Kedzie's 1874 Michigan book, Paris Green moving from parlour walls to potato fields (1867 on), state arsenic limits (New
   Hampshire, 1901), and the road to the 1906 Pure Food and Drug Act. That angle also makes the video useful for APUSH. Frame it as
   **"they knew, and kept selling it,"** with Morris's "witch fever" letter as the midpoint twist.
2. **Cold-open on Kedzie's book (a real artifact; channel rule),** with a stakes line in the first 10 seconds and an open loop that pays off
   at the end. Then cut straight to the logo. Aim for ≥60% of viewers still watching at 0:30 (vidIQ's benchmark).
3. **Run ~9:15–9:40 total,** over the 8-minute mid-roll line. Leave the length where the story ends; don't pad it.
4. **Publish Thursday 22 Oct 2026 at about 2:00 PM ET, as a scheduled public upload, not a Premiere.** That is nine days before Halloween,
   so the video goes into the spooky-season browse window, its 7-day Hype window covers the peak week, and teachers get a weekend to
   plan Halloween-week lessons. Hard fallback: Monday 26 Oct.
5. **Turn on Test & Compare at publish time with three titles and three thumbnails** (sets below). YouTube picks the winner by watch
   time, not CTR. Don't touch the title or thumbnail while the test runs, because any change ends it.
6. **Thumbnails: stand out by staying black and white.** Nearly every competitor thumbnail is soaked in green. A B&W archival frame with
   one coral subject and orange tape text will look different in that feed. Lead concept: the 1862 *Punch* "Arsenic Waltz" skeleton,
   tape text **DRESSED TO KILL**.
7. **Post four native vertical Shorts, one every other day from Fri 23 Oct to Fri 30 Oct,** each linked to the full video through
   "Related video." Plus one community Post before launch (a poll), one on launch day and one on Halloween.
8. **Pair the video with *Good Luck (Ouija)*.** Make a "Spooky History" playlist, use Ouija as the end-screen video, and add a link to
   the new video in the Ouija description and pinned comment for Halloween week. Ouija is the channel's natural October traffic source.
9. **Package for teachers:** chapters a teacher can show on their own (the U.S. chapter runs about 2 minutes), a 10–12 question viewing
   guide linked in the description, and an APUSH Periods 6–7 / chemistry line in the description. Then share it once in teacher
   communities.
10. **Fact-proof the comments section.** Leave out or flag the overused myths: Napoleon "killed by wallpaper," "arsine gas" as settled
    fact, Bradford 1858 as a green-pigment case (it was white arsenic), Scheele "killed by his own green," and Kedzie's book "killing
    readers." Debunking one (the gas theory) on screen is a retention beat, and it builds trust with teachers.

## 2. Search demand

**Google web search (US, 5 years):** "scheele's green" runs at about 1/10 the interest of "black sox scandal" and is in the same range as
"ouija board history." Searches for "arsenic wallpaper" and "victorian arsenic" are tiny. "Paris green" mostly means other things (a
band, places, paint colours).

- "scheele's green" monthly pattern: **no strong October spike** (Oct ≈ 1.6 vs. a yearly average of ≈ 1.4 on the scale used). The
  biggest week (Dec 24–30, 2023) matches *Stuff You Should Know*'s "Short Stuff: Scheele's Green" episode (27–28 Dec 2023). Media
  coverage drives demand, not the season.
- **On YouTube search**, every niche phrase ("scheele's green", "arsenic wallpaper", "arsenic green", "victorian arsenic") is below
  Trends' reporting threshold most weeks. Broader "arsenic" on YouTube *does* rise in October (Oct average ≈ 0.7 vs. ≈ 0.1 in other
  months; its top weeks all fall in October). That rise is partly *Arsenic and Old Lace* Halloween viewing. "halloween history" on
  YouTube peaks in the last week of October every year.
- **What this means:** views will come mainly from **browse and suggested** (spooky-season viewing next to other dark-history videos),
  not search. Search terms still matter for the long tail, so put one keyword in the title and the main phrases in the first two
  description lines.

**Autocomplete (what people type):**
- YouTube: `scheele's green`, `scheele's green idv` (an *Identity V* game cosmetic, a gamer collision; ignore it), `arsenic green`,
  `arsenic green paint`, `arsenic green dress`, `green arsenic books`, `victorian arsenic green`, `arsenic victorian era`,
  `shadows from the walls of death (book)`, `arsenic in candy`.
- Google: `scheele's green vs paris green`, `scheele's green wallpaper`, `scheele's green napoleon`, `arsenic wallpaper victorian`,
  `arsenic wallpaper symptoms`, `arsenic wallpaper controversy`, `paris green poison`, `paris green pesticide` / `insecticide`,
  `victorian arsenic book`, `victorian arsenic dress`, `arsenic green books`.
- Takeaways: (a) **"arsenic green"** and **"scheele's green"** are the two head terms; (b) people ask **"vs. Paris green"**, so define
  the difference in one line (Scheele's = copper arsenite, 1775; Paris/emerald green = copper aceto-arsenite, about 1814, brighter and
  more durable); (c) **"paris green pesticide"** is a real query, which supports the U.S. farm beat; (d) **"green arsenic books"** is
  live (old cloth bindings), which could make a pinned-comment fact or a Short.
- **Common misspellings** (the one case where YouTube says tags help): `scheeles green`, `sheeles green`, "Shields green" in podcast
  transcripts.

**TikTok/Shorts:** the topic comes back every October (#ScaryFashionMonth tag; "deadliest green" Shorts in the millions). Short-form
demand is proven; long-form demand is thin and crowded with low-quality uploads.

## 3. Format: retention for a sub-10-minute history video

### What YouTube itself says (evidence)

- **The first 30 seconds are measured directly.** Studio's "Intro" metric is "what percentage of your audience still watched your
  video after the first 30 seconds." YouTube's own fixes are to make the thumbnail and title match the content, and to "modify the first
  30 seconds… and experiment with different styles." **[YouTube]** ([Help: key moments](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en))
- **Front-load the good part.** If top moments come late, "consider introducing the compelling content earlier in the video."
  **[YouTube]** (same page)
- **Length:** there is no ideal length. YouTube's Creator Liaison says a video should be "as long as it needs to be, but no longer," and
  that padding or over-cutting both lose viewers. **[YouTube, secondhand]** ([vidIQ on length](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/best-youtube-video-length/))
- **Mid-rolls need 8:00 or more.** "On monetized videos that are 8 minutes or longer, you can turn on ads during the middle of the
  video." Automatic slots look for "natural visual or audio breaks." **[YouTube]** ([Help: mid-roll](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6175006?hl=en))
- **No single metric decides distribution, and the first hour doesn't settle it.** Todd Beaupré (YouTube's head of growth and
  discovery), Creator Insider, Sept 2026: "We're not basing decisions just on those initial moments"; "No metric on its own is a good
  indicator of value"; and subscribers decide only a small share of views ("90% of the time, your subscribed audience isn't deciding to
  watch"). **[YouTube]** ([PPC Land summary](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/))
  So upload hour matters less than folklore says; packaging and satisfaction matter more.
- **Chapters:** first timestamp 0:00, at least 3, each at least 10 s. **[YouTube]** ([Help: chapters](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579?hl=en))

### Creator-education guidance (informed opinion)

- **Paddy Galloway:** the packaging (title and thumbnail) sets the promise and the script must keep it; write and test "three or four
  different intro versions"; find the "retention danger zones" in past videos; titles "50 characters or under"; thumbnail text "over
  five or six words" is too much; test "very different concepts." ([Creator Science #152](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/paddy-galloway/); [Galloway on open loops](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/paddy-galloway-459b8913a_what-do-these-four-viral-youtube-videos-have-activity-7444402853705854978-IsL1))
- **vidIQ:** keep ≥60% of viewers past 0:30 (≥65% is strong) and use your own trailing average as the real baseline.
  ([vidIQ retention](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/))
- **Re-hooks every 60–90 s, pattern interrupts, "open loops":** widely taught (MrBeast-style escalation, scriptwriting courses),
  **but [folklore] as fixed rules.** Nobody has published evidence for a fixed interval. The evidence-based version is to read your
  own retention graph. The channel's earlier videos (Ouija, Black Sox) are the best guide to where *this* audience drops.
- **Folklore to ignore:** "the algorithm rewards 8+ minutes" (it rewards satisfied watch time; 8:00 is only an ad rule); "first hour
  makes or breaks it" (contradicted by Beaupré); "post at exactly X o'clock" (published studies disagree with one another).

### Applied to this video

- **First 30 s:** one real object (Kedzie's book), one stake ("every page is poison, and every page was for sale"), one open loop (the
  man behind the world's most famous wallpaper said the doctors had "witch fever"). No greeting, no channel intro before 0:30.
- **A turn every 60–90 s** written into the script as a *new question* rather than a cliffhanger stinger: "Scheele knew." → "It wasn't
  just walls." → "Doctors could test for it." → "Guess whose family owned the mine." → "Meanwhile, in America…" → "The same green went
  onto dinner."
- **Pattern interrupts the channel already owns:** logo breaks between chapters, word-by-word quotes (Morris's letter), a recurring
  device (see the "86 samples" counter below), a drawn diagram (dust vs. gas), and parallax archival photos.
- **Payoff timing:** answer the cold-open question in two stages: Morris at about 50%, Kedzie's book at about 70%. That keeps a
  reason to stay into the back half. Save the closing line for the return to the book.
- **End screen:** 12–15 s, music only (channel practice), with the narrated thank-you before it. Elements: Subscribe plus *Good Luck
  (Ouija)* as a fixed video (a Halloween pair), not "best for viewer."

### Test & Compare: how to use it

**How it works [YouTube]:** up to 3 titles, 3 thumbnails, or 3 title-and-thumbnail combinations per long-form video; desktop Studio
only; needs advanced features. YouTube shows the options evenly and **picks the winner by watch time** (not CTR). Results read
"Winner," "Performed Same" or "Inconclusive"; inconclusive keeps the first option. Usually done within 2 weeks. **Changing the title or
thumbnail mid-test stops it.** Not available for Premieres until they end, or for made-for-kids, private or mature videos.
([Help: Test & Compare](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=en); [Tubefilter, Jul 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/16/youtube-feature-test-and-compare-titles-thumbnails/))

**Plan:** start a **combined title-and-thumbnail test at publish**: (A) title 1 + thumb A, (B) title 2 + thumb B, (C) title 3 + thumb C.
Three *very different* concepts, per Galloway. After it finishes (about 5 Nov), if A wins, optionally run a second, thumbnail-only test
of A vs. F (coral vs. green accent) to settle the brand question with data.

## 5. Release timing

- **Date: Thursday 22 Oct 2026.** Why:
  - "Halloween history" interest on YouTube peaks in the **last week of October**. Publishing about nine days ahead lets the video
    collect early viewers and get placed by the recommender before that peak, and it is still fresh during the week itself.
  - **Hype** (channels under 500K subscribers) works only during a video's first 7 days, so 22–29 Oct covers the peak week. Hypes
    put the video on a weekly leaderboard but don't change recommendations. ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-hype/); [Tubefilter, Aug 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/08/26/youtube-hype-feature-launches-united-states-global/))
  - **Teachers** plan on the weekend. A Thursday upload is findable for Halloween-week lessons (26–30 Oct).
  - Fallbacks: Tue 20 Oct (if it's ready early), or Mon 26 Oct (the latest worth doing; after that the seasonal lift is mostly gone).
    Because "scheele's green" search isn't seasonal, a slip costs the Halloween browse bonus, not the video's long-tail value.
- **Time: about 2:00 PM ET.** Published studies point to weekday afternoons, but they disagree with each other ([Buffer via roundups](https://quso.ai/blog/best-time-to-post-on-youtube.html);
  [IQFluence, 325 campaigns](https://iqfluence.io/blog/best-time-to-post-on-youtube)), and YouTube says the first hour doesn't decide
  a video's fate. Check Studio → Audience → "When your viewers are on YouTube" and post about 1–2 hours before your own peak.
- **Premiere or publish: publish (scheduled).** At this channel's size a Premiere adds a countdown page and chat but little else;
  creator guides say small channels gain little ([Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-premiere/)). Premieres also can't run
  Test & Compare until the premiere ends.

### Educational angles (for teachers to share)

- **APUSH Period 6 (1865–1898):** industrial consumer goods reach ordinary homes; new **state boards of health** (Michigan's dates to
  1873; Kedzie chaired its committee on poisons); experts vs. manufacturers.
- **APUSH Period 7 (1890–1945):** the Progressive-Era move from "buyer beware" to regulation; state per-square-yard arsenic limits
  (New Hampshire, 1901); the 1906 Pure Food and Drug Act (food and drugs, not wallpaper; say so plainly). Dr. Harvey Wiley's "Poison
  Squad" is the bridge. Name the concept and define it: **consumer protection**.
- **AP World Unit 5–6 (industrialization):** synthetic pigments, global chemistry, mining (Devon Great Consols).
- **Chemistry / AP Chem / APES:** copper arsenite vs. copper aceto-arsenite; why the green is green; the Marsh test (1836), which meant
  arsenic *could* be detected; volatile arsenic and the "gas" myth; arsenical pesticide residues still in orchard soils.
- **Teacher demand:** about three-quarters of U.S. K–12 teachers use YouTube for instruction, mostly on a whole-class screen, for about
  18% of instructional time ([Carpenter et al., *Educational Researcher*, 2026](https://www.elon.edu/u/news/?p=1050454)). Help them by
  (1) making the U.S. chapter (5:10–7:00) work on its own, (2) linking a 10–12 question viewing guide (Explain / Describe / Identify,
  "way more simple"), and (3) keeping the video unrestricted, with no gore and no on-screen corpses.
- **Sharing (once each, no spam):** the channel's own Post; r/APUSH and teacher Facebook groups ("free 9-minute Halloween-week video
  plus viewing guide," with a link to the guide, not just the video); chemistry-teacher groups with the "chemistry of a killer
  pigment" framing.

### Myths and fact-check flags for the script

- **Napoleon:** speculative; one line at most, framed as "still argued."
- **"Arsine/mould gas killed them":** contested; present it as the period theory and the modern doubt
  ([Cullen & Bentley 2005](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2005/em/b413752n); [Trimethylarsine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trimethylarsine)).
- **Bradford 1858:** arsenic trioxide (white) mixed into sweets by mistake, not a green-pigment case. Mention it only as "arsenic was
  everywhere."
- **Scheele "killed by his own green":** unproven; skip it.
- **Kedzie's book "killed readers" / "Guinness record":** uncorroborated. Guinness ran a feature, not a formal record
  ([Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2024/1/why-an-american-doctor-wrote-the-most-poisonous-book-ever-763382)).
  The counts of surviving copies differ (4 known, plus a fifth found at the Michigan History Center:
  [Michiganology](https://michiganology.org/stories/shadows-from-the-walls-of-death)).
- **"Two-thirds of U.S. wallpaper contained arsenic":** a single secondary source; don't use it unless a primary source is found.
- **Turner children:** sources say 3 or 4 siblings; check against the inquest report.
- **Morris's "witch fever" wording:** varies by source; quote from Hawksley or the Morris letters edition.
  ([Nature 2003](https://www.nature.com/articles/423688a); [Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/opinion/morriss-arsenic-entanglement/3005031.article))
