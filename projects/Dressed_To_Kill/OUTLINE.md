# Arsenic Green: beat outline (draft 1)

**15 Minute History** · target **about 9:30 finished** (about 1,600 words of narration at the locked pace, plus intro, logo breaks and a 12 s end screen)
**Frame:** a British story with a U.S. thread (about 40% American). Wry throughout, one heavy chapter (ch04), a few AI paintings for gaps.

**Driving question (cold open):** *Everybody knew arsenic was poison. So how did it end up on the walls, in the dresses and on the candy, and why did it take so long to get it off?*

**Answer (last chapter):** Nobody had to prove it was safe. The evidence was messy (not everyone got sick, the doctors argued about *how* it poisoned people), the trade was huge, and the people profiting called it panic. In Britain there was never a ban: shoppers, scared by doctors, newspapers and *Punch*, simply stopped buying, and "arsenic-free" became a selling point. In America a state health report said in 1872 that a ban was impossible "under a government organized like our own"; Massachusetts finally set a legal limit in 1900. The green never changed. What changed was who had to prove it was safe.

**Working title options** (on-screen title · subtitle):
1. **Witch Fever** · *The Victorian Craze for Poison Green* (Morris's own word for the scare; reads as Halloween without being cheesy)
2. **Shall We Poison Our Homes?** · *The Arsenic Green Craze, 1775–1900* (Kedzie's own headline)
3. **Dressed to Kill** · *How Arsenic Green Took Over the 1800s* (pairs with the Arsenic Waltz thumbnail)
