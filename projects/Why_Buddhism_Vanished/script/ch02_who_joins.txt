Every religion answers this question, even if it never says so out loud.

Some religions say: our message is for everyone, everywhere. Geographers call these universalizing religions. They welcome converts, and many actively send people out to share their beliefs. So they tend to travel far from home.

Other religions are tied to a specific people and a specific place. You're usually born into them, through your family and culture. These are ethnic religions. They rarely look for converts, so they tend to stay clustered near the place they began.

That birthplace has a name: the hearth. And how a religion moves out from its hearth is called diffusion. It can happen three main ways.

People can pack up and move, carrying their faith with them. That's relocation diffusion. A belief can spread from person to person, neighbor to neighbor, like something going viral. That's contagious diffusion. Or it can start at the top, with a king or an emperor, and flow down through everyone they rule. That's hierarchical diffusion.

Hold onto those. Because each of these three religions spread in a different way, and that's what explains the map.
