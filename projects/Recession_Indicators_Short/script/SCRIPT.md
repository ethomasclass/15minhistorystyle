## Script

> Can men's underwear predict a recession? People have actually tried. And before underwear, they tried skirts.
>
> The 1920s were booming, and skirts got short. Then the Great Depression hit, and hemlines dropped. The theory: short skirts, good times. Long skirts, trouble. In 2010, researchers checked ninety years of fashion magazines. *It didn't hold up.*
>
> Skyscrapers. The Empire State Building opened in 1931, in the Depression. It was so empty, people called it the "Empty State Building." Some say record-tall buildings always open before a crash. Statisticians say: *coincidence.*
>
> Lipstick. After 9/11, sales jumped 11%. Small treats in hard times. But in 2008? They *fell* almost 6%.
>
> And underwear. When money's tight, men stop buying new ones. A Federal Reserve chairman reportedly watched it. In 2009, sales were on track to drop for the first time since anyone started tracking them.
>
> So, can underwear predict a recession? Once, maybe. But one lucky guess isn't a forecast.

## Fact-check flags
0. **The 1920s and 1930s story.** Hemlines rose to about the knee in the mid-1920s boom and fell back to mid-calf in the early-1930s Depression. That's the observation the hemline theory grew from. The script calls it "the theory," not a fact.
1. **Hemline Index, "tested in 2010… didn't hold up" / "ninety years of fashion magazines."** (*L'Officiel*, 1921–2009.) Franses and van Baardwijk (Erasmus University, 2010) coded hemlines from the fashion magazine *L'Officiel* back to 1921 against economic cycle data from the National Bureau of Economic Research, and called the index an urban legend. At most, fashion lags the economy by about three years.
2. **Lipstick +11% after 9/11.** Leonard Lauder (Estée Lauder) cited an 11% rise in U.S. lipstick sales after September 11, 2001.
3. **Lipstick fell almost 6% in 2008.** The market researcher Kline & Company reported U.S. lipstick sales fell 5.8% in 2008. Kline reported another −5.3% in 2009; NPD put 2009 at nearly −10%. "The worst recession in decades" is the Great Recession (Dec 2007 – June 2009).
4. **Skyscrapers.** The Empire State Building opened May 1, 1931, about 75% empty (less than 25% rented). The "Empty State" nickname was in print by March 1932 (Zanesville *Times Recorder*, cited by Barry Popik). It didn't turn a profit until around the 1950s. The "Skyscraper Index" (Andrew Lawrence, 1999) found no reliable statistical support in later tests (Barr, Mizrach and Mundra, 2015), hence "coincidence."
5. **Underwear.** Reported as a favorite indicator of Alan Greenspan (secondhand, hence "reportedly"). The market researcher Mintel projected a 2.3% drop in U.S. men's underwear sales for 2009, the first decline since it began tracking in 2003 (as reported by the Washington Post, NBC News and HuffPost in 2009). It was a projection, hence "on track to fall."
6. **"Once, maybe."** The 2009 underwear dip lined up with a recession; the other three don't hold up. This is the honest summary, not a claim that underwear works.
