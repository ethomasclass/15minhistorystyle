Economists have tried to predict recessions with skirts, lipstick, and men's underwear. Let's see how that went.

The Hemline Index: short skirts mean good times, long skirts mean trouble. When researchers finally tested it in 2010? *Urban legend.*

Lipstick. In 2001, the head of Estée Lauder noticed lipstick sales jumping in hard times. The theory: skip the dress, buy a small treat. Since then? Hit or miss.

Underwear. Even a Federal Reserve chairman reportedly watched men's underwear sales. When money's tight, men stop replacing it. More than we needed to know.

Skyscrapers. Record-tall buildings keep opening right as the economy falls. The Empire State Building, 1931. Dubai's Burj Khalifa, 2010. Statisticians call it *coincidence.*

So does anything work? Economists' favorite is the yield curve. When short-term loans pay more than long-term ones, a recession usually follows. It's predicted almost every one since the 1960s. Then it flashed red in 2022, longer than ever.

And the recession? *Still waiting.*
