Can you predict a recession by looking at… men's underwear?

People have tried.

Skirts. The idea: short skirts mean good times, long skirts mean bad times. In 2010, researchers tested it. It didn't work.

Lipstick. The idea: in hard times, people still buy small treats, like lipstick. Sometimes true. Often not.

Underwear. The idea: when money's tight, men stop buying new ones. A Federal Reserve chairman reportedly watched it. *Too much information.*

Skyscrapers. The idea: record-tall buildings open right as the economy crashes. The Empire State Building, 1931. Statisticians say: *coincidence.*

So what actually works? Economists watch the yield curve. Normally, if you lend money for longer, you get paid more. When that flips, investors are scared. Since the 1960s, that's come before almost every recession. But in 2022, it flipped, and no recession came. Not yet.

So, can you predict a recession? Not with underwear. The best tool we have is right most of the time. Not always.
