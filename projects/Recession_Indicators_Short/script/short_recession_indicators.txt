Can men's underwear predict a recession? People have actually tried. And before underwear, they tried skirts.

The 1920s were booming, and skirts got short. Then the Great Depression hit, and hemlines dropped. The theory: short skirts, good times. Long skirts, trouble. In 2010, researchers checked ninety years of fashion magazines. *It didn't hold up.*

Skyscrapers. The Empire State Building opened in 1931, in the Depression. It was so empty, people called it the "Empty State Building." Some say record-tall buildings always open before a crash. Statisticians say: *coincidence.*

Lipstick. After 9/11, sales jumped 11%. Small treats in hard times. But in 2008? They *fell* almost 6%.

And underwear. When money's tight, men stop buying new ones. A Federal Reserve chairman reportedly watched it. In 2009, sales were on track to drop for the first time since anyone started tracking them.

So, can underwear predict a recession? Once, maybe. But one lucky guess isn't a forecast.
