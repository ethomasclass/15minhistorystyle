1821. Mexico wins its independence from Spain, and inherits a huge northern frontier called Texas.

The Comanche and other Native nations live there. But Mexico's own settlers, the {Tejanos}, Mexican Texans, number only a few thousand. So Mexico has an idea. Invite people in. Give them cheap land, and they'll farm it and help defend it.

Americans jump at the deal. Stephen F. Austin, an {empresario}, a contractor paid in land for bringing in settlers, brings the first 300 families. The rules: become a Mexican citizen, follow Mexican law, and become Catholic.

Many of the settlers come from the South to grow cotton. And they bring enslaved people with them.

By the early 1830s, Americans in Texas outnumber Tejanos many times over. Mexico's plan worked. A little too well.

So Mexico tries to slow things down. In 1829, it *abolishes slavery*. Texas talks its way into an exception.

In 1830, Mexico bans new American settlers and new enslaved people, and sends soldiers to collect new taxes on imports. Americans keep coming anyway. Something has to give.
