# Gemini image prompts: The Exact Spot

These cover only moments with no surviving picture. **Draft 2 adds three (17–19) for chapter 6.** Real people (Lincoln, Polk, Santa Anna, Austin, Houston, Seguín, Taylor, Scott,
Trist, Grant, Wilmot, Emerson) come from archival portraits (list at the bottom). **No prompt shows a recognizable real person.** Where
a real person is in the scene, they're seen from behind, at a distance or in shadow.

## How to run them

- **Model:** Gemini image generation (Nano Banana Pro), at the largest size it offers. **Aspect ratio:** 16:9 for every image.
- **Paste the whole block, including the Style paragraph.** Keeping it identical is what makes the images look like one series.
- **Send them back** as PNGs named as shown (for example `ch03_come_and_take_it.png`). Drop them in this chat or in
  `projects/Mexican_American_War/public/img/gen/`.
- **Regenerate rather than fix** anything with text, extra fingers, modern objects, or a wrong uniform or building.
- **Must-have** images carry a scene. Every other scene still works if you skip its image (a map, document or drawn graphic takes its
  place).
- **Mask pass** (where listed): once you like an image, upload that same image back with the mask prompt and send me both. Name the
  magenta copy `<name>_mask.png`. It puts the coral tint and teal outline exactly on the subject.

**Style** (goes at the end of every prompt below, word for word):

> Style: an American oil painting from the 1830s–1840s in the manner of the period's genre and frontier painters, such as George Caleb Bingham and William Sidney Mount. Muted earth palette (umber, ochre, slate blue, dull red), warm directional light, visible brushwork, faint craquelure, slightly aged varnish. Every detail must be historically accurate for the stated year and place: clothing, hairstyles, uniforms, weapons, buildings, furniture, lamps and tools. No text of any kind: no letters, words, signs, labels, captions, signatures, dates or watermarks. No frame or border. No recognizable real historical person. Faces, where visible, belong to ordinary anonymous people.

**Mask pass** (fill in the subject named under each image):

> Return this exact same image, pixel-aligned, with no other change at all, except: fill the silhouette of [SUBJECT] completely with flat pure magenta (#FF00FF). Keep the fill tight to its outline. Do not move, crop, recompose or redraw anything else.

## Chapter 2 · Come On In

### 1. `ch02_settlers_arrive.png` · must-have
*Covers:* "Americans jump at the deal… Many of the settlers come from the South to grow cotton. And they bring enslaved people with them."
> A wagon road through open grassland and scattered live oaks in Mexican Texas, near the Brazos River, spring 1825. A small party of American settlers moves west in two canvas-covered ox wagons: a man in a broad-brimmed hat and linen hunting shirt walking beside the oxen, a woman in a calico dress and sunbonnet on the wagon seat with a small child. Walking behind the second wagon, carrying bundles and tools, are four enslaved Black men and women in plain homespun clothing, shown with dignity as individuals, faces tired and watchful, not caricatured. Bright, hazy morning light, tall green grass, a wide pale sky. Quiet, matter-of-fact, not heroic.
> *[Style]*
> *Mask pass:* the enslaved men and women walking behind the second wagon.

### 2. `ch02_tejano_ranch.png`
*Covers:* "Mexico's own settlers, the Tejanos, Mexican Texans, number only a few thousand."
> A Tejano ranch outside San Antonio de Béxar, 1825. A low, whitewashed adobe and stone ranch house with a flat roof and wooden vigas, a ramada of mesquite poles covered in brush, a corral of upright mesquite posts. A Tejano rancher in a short dark jacket, wide-brimmed flat-crowned hat and leather leggings sits on a horse with a Mexican saddle; his wife in a long skirt and rebozo stands in the doorway; two children play near a carreta (a two-wheeled oxcart with solid wooden wheels). Late afternoon light, long shadows, dry brush country and a line of cottonwoods along a creek.

Above them, on a pole, a plain white cotton flag painted with a single black cannon and a single black five-pointed star, and nothing else. Across the river, through morning mist, a small line of Mexican dragoons in blue coatees and tall shakos sits on horseback. Tense, quiet standoff, river fog, low gold sunlight.

## Chapter 4 · Remember the Alamo

### 6. `ch04_alamo_dawn.png` · must-have
*Covers:* "March 6th, before dawn, Santa Anna attacks."
> The old Spanish mission of the Alamo in San Antonio, before dawn, March 6, 1836. The roofless limestone chapel with its scarred, unfinished façade (no curved top, which was added later) stands behind earth-and-timber walls and a long low barrack. Seen from a distance across open ground: columns of Mexican infantry in dark blue coatees and white trousers, carrying ladders and muskets, advancing through the blue-grey half-light toward the walls. Musket and cannon smoke drifting, flashes of fire along the walls. No close-up violence, no bodies. Cold, dramatic, the moment before the end.
> *[Style]*
> *Mask pass:* the chapel.

### 7. `ch04_marsh_grass.png`
*Covers:* "Texans find a man hiding in the marsh grass, dressed as a private."
> Marsh grass and a muddy bayou near the San Jacinto River, Texas, the morning of April 22, 1836. Three Texian soldiers in hunting shirts and slouch hats, rifles in hand, have just parted the tall reeds. Crouched in the grass in front of them, seen entirely from behind, is a man in a plain, ill-fitting blue Mexican private's jacket and white trousers, his face turned away and hidden. The Texians' faces show surprise and suspicion. Soft, misty morning light, reeds glinting with water.
> *[Style]*
> *Mask pass:* the crouching man.

## Chapter 6 · Plot Twists

### 17. `ch06_blockade.png` · must-have
*Covers:* "Polk says yes. The U.S. Navy lets him sail right past."
> The Gulf of Mexico off Veracruz, a bright hot morning in August 1846. In the foreground, a British paddle-wheel mail steamer with a tall black funnel and two masts steams toward the low white city of Veracruz and the stone fortress of San Juan de Ulúa on the horizon. Close by, a U.S. Navy sailing frigate of the blockade squadron lies at anchor with its gun ports open, sailors in white frocks and dark hats lining the rail, simply watching it pass. On the steamer's deck, one gentleman in a dark frock coat and top hat stands at the rail with his back to the viewer, face not visible, looking toward shore. Blue-green water, gulls, heat haze.
> *[Style]*
> *Mask pass:* the British steamer.

### 18. `ch06_cork_leg.png` · must-have
*Covers:* "Then in 1847, soldiers from Illinois captured his cork leg on the battlefield."
> A rocky mountain road near Cerro Gordo, Mexico, April 18, 1847, just after a battle. An abandoned, elegant black carriage with a broken wheel sits tilted at the roadside, its door open. Three American volunteer soldiers in sky-blue wool jackets, white cross-belts and forage caps (ordinary anonymous young men from Illinois) gather by the door; one holds up a 19th-century artificial leg made of cork and wood with a laced leather socket and brass fittings, and the others laugh in disbelief. A roast chicken and a toppled bottle lie on the carriage seat. Scrubby hills, drifting smoke in the distance, bright afternoon light. Comic, but not cartoonish.
> *[Style]*
> *Mask pass:* the artificial leg.

### 19. `ch06_flag_and_gallows.png` · optional (heavy, teal only, no mask)

In the middle distance, a long row of army mule wagons stands beneath a long rough timber beam on posts; on the wagons stand a line of small, dark, indistinct standing figures, too far away to see any faces, guarded by ranks of soldiers in sky-blue uniforms. Far away on the horizon, on a steep wooded hill, the stone castle of Chapultepec is wreathed in grey battle smoke. No hanging bodies, no violence shown, nothing close up. Pale, hazy, cold light; a sense of waiting and dread.
