So who paid for all this?

About 1,700 Americans were killed in battle. More than 11,500 died of disease: dysentery, yellow fever, measles. For every soldier killed in battle, nearly 7 died of sickness.

Mexico's losses were larger. No one counted them all.

About 80,000 Mexicans lived in the Mexican Cession. They didn't move. The border did.

On paper, their property was protected. But in 1851, Congress made landowners in California prove their titles in court. Many cases took more than 15 years. Lawyers' fees and squatters took much of the land anyway.

Native nations weren't part of the treaty at all. No one asked them. In Texas, tens of thousands of enslaved people were now held in an American state. No one asked them either.

And Juan Seguín, the Tejano who rode out of the Alamo for help? He became mayor of San Antonio. Then newer Anglo settlers accused him of siding with Mexico, and drove him out. Under pressure, he ended up fighting for Mexico.

Years later, he called himself "a foreigner in my native land."
