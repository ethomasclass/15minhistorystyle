Where exactly did the Mexican-American War begin? In 1846, President James K. Polk told Congress that Mexico had "shed American blood upon the American soil." A young congressman named Abraham Lincoln asked him to name the exact spot. This is the story behind that question: Texas, the Texas Revolution, the annexation of Texas, Manifest Destiny, and the war that cost Mexico half its land.

This is the Mexican-American War explained in 15 minutes: Mexican Texas and Stephen F. Austin, the Texas Revolution and the Alamo, Polk and the disputed land between the Nueces and the Rio Grande, the Thornton Affair, the war itself, the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo and the Mexican Cession, and the Wilmot Proviso that brought slavery back to the center of American politics. Along the way we look at both sides: supporters who saw a defensive war and America's destiny, and critics who saw a president who sent an army into disputed land and got the war he wanted.
