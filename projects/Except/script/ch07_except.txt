Now the amendment. Remember, the Emancipation Proclamation was a war measure. It didn't reach the border states, and once the war ended, a court might throw it out. To end slavery for good, it had to go into the Constitution.

The Senate passed the {13th Amendment} in April 1864, 38 to 6. The House took longer. It finally passed on January 31st, 1865, 119 to 56, and people in the gallery cheered.

Then three-fourths of the states had to ratify it, or approve it. The state that put it over the top, on December 6th, 1865, was Georgia.

Slavery was over in the United States. All of the nearly 4 million people once enslaved were now legally free.

But read it again. "Except as a punishment for crime."

Even before the amendment was ratified, Southern states began passing {Black Codes}, laws aimed only at Black Americans. In Mississippi, a Black person without a job could be arrested and fined, and if they couldn't pay, hired out to work for whoever paid the fine. Arrest someone for a crime, and that exception could put them back to forced work. Later, under {convict leasing}, states rented out prisoners to plantations, mines and railroads.

Freedom was in the Constitution. What freedom would mean was another fight.
