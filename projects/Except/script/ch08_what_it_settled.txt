So let's go back to the question. How was the war decided?

Mostly on the battlefield. Gettysburg ended Lee's invasions. Vicksburg cut the Confederacy in two. Atlanta saved Lincoln's presidency. And the people who carried the war were often the ones with the least say: poor men who couldn't pay 300 dollars, Black soldiers paid less to fight, and civilians in the army's path.

What did it settle? Two big things. No state could leave the Union. And slavery was over, written into the Constitution, where no president or court could easily undo it.

What did it leave open? Almost everything else. Were freed people citizens? Could they vote? Who would protect their rights, Washington or the states?

The war answered whether the country would stay together. It didn't answer what kind of country it would be.

That fight is called Reconstruction, the years when the country tried to rebuild the South and decide what freedom would mean. And it started with six words. Except as a punishment for crime.
