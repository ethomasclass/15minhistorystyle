That November, Lincoln traveled to Gettysburg to help dedicate a cemetery for the Union dead.

The main speaker talked for about two hours. Lincoln spoke for about two minutes. Around 270 words.

He started with a date. "Four score and seven years ago." A score is 20. So, 87 years before 1863. That's 1776. Not the Constitution. The Declaration of Independence, and its promise that "all men are created equal."

Then he said what the living owed the dead: to make sure the nation would have "a new birth of freedom."

Notice what changed. Two years earlier, the war was about holding the Union together. Now Lincoln was saying it was about *finishing what 1776 started.*

Five copies of the speech in Lincoln's handwriting survive, and no two are exactly alike. So which one is the real speech? Good question. There was no recording, and even the reporters wrote it down differently.
