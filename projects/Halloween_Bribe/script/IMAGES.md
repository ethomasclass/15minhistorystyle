# Paid to Stop: archival image picks

Sourced 2026-10-27 for the 10-minute cut in `OUTLINE.md`. Everything lives under `public/img/chNN/` and has an entry in
`public/img/credits.json` (keyed by the path under `public/img/`). Everything goes on screen black and white, so the colour
originals (postcards, posters) are fine. Contact sheet: `review/archival_sheet.jpg`.

**File-name flags**
- `FAIRUSE_…`: copyrighted or rights unknown. Short on-screen use with a credit, only while the narration comments on it, or replace.
- `CCBY_…`: Creative Commons (attribution required in the description). Not public domain; use only if you accept CC BY.
- Every other file is public domain (reason in its row).

**Newspaper crops** (`ch03/`, `ch04/`) come from LOC Chronicling America via `tools/phrase_find.py`. Each has a `.json` beside it
with the line box of the phrase (for `Swipe` / `Spot`). Rights: LOC says there are no known restrictions on the Chronicling
America pages and the researcher is responsible for rights. The 1930s–40s titles used here (Bismarck Tribune, Indianapolis Times,
Imperial Valley Press, Tacoma Times, Washington Daily News, Browning Chief) are treated as public domain because their copyright
wasn't renewed. That is the usual reading for these titles, but it hasn't been checked one by one. Wire copy (AP/UP) is the same.

## The hook: wagon on the roof (fact-check flag 8)

**No public-domain wagon-on-a-roof photo was found.** Searched: LOC (P&P, Chronicling America), Wikimedia Commons, Internet
Archive, NYPL, Wisconsin Historical Society, plus a web search. What exists:

- **Univ. of Tennessee, "Prank (Halloween—wagon atop Jefferson Hall)", 1905**: a print in UT Special Collections (finding aid only,
  not digitized; [scout.lib.utk.edu](https://scout.lib.utk.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/126584)). Published or not, a 1905 photo
  is public domain by age once you get a scan. **Best lead for the real hook photo: ask UT Special Collections for a scan.**
  [Volopedia](https://volopedia.lib.utk.edu/entries/pranks-halloween/) also describes a 1908 delivery van on the Jefferson Hall roof,
  "the most photographed" prank of the era.
- `ch01/FAIRUSE_cleveland_press_halloween_prank_1955.jpg` (800×505): Cleveland Press photo, 1955. Objects hauled onto the roof of
  Noel Confectionery, with debris and signs strewn across the street. Source:
  [Cleveland Historical / CSU Special Collections](https://clevelandhistorical.org/files/show/6098). Rights not stated. Use it only as a
  fair-use card (low resolution), or ask CSU.
- PD fallback for frame 0: `ch02/postcard_halloween_pranks_boy_jackolantern_1911.jpg` or the 1904 Puck spread (below) with an
  **AI wood engraving / painting of the wagon on the barn roof** (see Gaps). The documented incidents to name: Beloit, Wis. (1888,
  "a wagon loaded with wood was carried to the top of the college building"), Newton, Kan. (1892), Knoxville (1905/1908).

  were looked at and dropped.

## Gaps: no good archival image (candidates for AI paintings / engravings)

1. **The wagon on the barn roof** (hook, thumbnail, ch7 reversal). Must-have unless UT Special Collections supplies the 1905 print.
   Prompt idea: night after Halloween, c. 1900, Midwest farm; a farm wagon reassembled on the ridge of a barn roof; dawn light;
   a farmer and his wife looking up from the yard. Wood-engraving style matches the Doyle/Kelley plates.
2. **Turnip lanterns in use**: children in rural Ireland/Scotland c. 1880 carrying glowing carved turnips at night. (The museum cast is
   a photo of an object only.)
3. **Halloween bonfires** (Samhain/Hallowe'en fires on Irish/Scottish hillsides, 19th c.): no PD photo or engraving found.
4. **Greased streetcar tracks / a streetcar stuck, youths on a 1930s city street at night.** The FSA streetcar photos are daytime.
5. **Teen pranksters c. 1900–1930 at work** (gate off its hinges, soaping a window, tipping an outhouse). Only aftermath images exist.
6. **1950s candy ads** (Mars, Hershey, Brach's Halloween ads): all in copyrighted magazines; none cleared. Draw generic period candy or
   use a drawn bowl.
7. **Hospital X-raying a trick-or-treat bag, 1980s**: copyrighted wire photos only (see ch6).
8. **The "two views" card (ch7)**: a designed card, not an archive image.

**What the 1 Nov 1933 story says (OCR, checked):** "SIX POLICE CLASH WITH GANG OF BOYS NEAR HIGH SCHOOL". One boy wounded: George
Manning, 17, hit in the legs by shot from a .410 that he said "an irate woman" fired (she said she aimed at the sidewalk). "Police authorities
used tear gas bombs and cartridges to disperse a large crowd of pranksters… one of the wildest Halloween nights in the history of
Bismarck." **About 300 boys at the high school**, who had piled "several old automobiles… garbage cans, old boats and… rubbish onto the
steps of the high school building". Police were called just after 10 p.m.: three patrolmen, Sheriff Joseph L. Kelley and two deputies. The
mob tried to turn over the sheriff's car and broke a window; James Wolfer, 24, a capitol construction worker, was charged. A 150-ft picket fence
was torn down; "the usual tipping over of out-houses, soaping of windows". Pranks went on until about 1:30 a.m. Same story, other
cities: Fargo (a house set afire), Minot, Valley City (a girl hurt when wagon wheels tied to a car by pranksters ran wild), **St. Paul: "the
most destructive Halloween in 20 years", $3,000–4,000 damage to street lights, 166 calls in seven hours, 300 officers on foot, 30 boys
held, 22 false alarms.** Follow-up, *Bismarck Tribune* 2 Nov 1933 p. 1, "Prankster Fined by Police Magistrate": Wolfer fined $5 for the
window, "as the sheriff assisted city patrolmen in dispersing a crowd of 300 Halloween pranksters. Tear gas bombs were used to bring
the boys to order." It confirms the 300 figure. Not cropped (LOC Cloudflare challenge): `--phrase "crowd of 300 Halloween pranksters"`.
