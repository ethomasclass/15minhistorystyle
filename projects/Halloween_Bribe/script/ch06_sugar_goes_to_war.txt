This is a ration book from World War Two. In the spring of 1942, sugar became the first food America {rationed}, meaning each family could only buy a set amount. And it was the last food to come off the list, in 1947. That's five Halloweens with hardly any candy.

So Halloween got pulled way back. Kids were told that pranks helped the enemy. The soap you'd smear on a window, the grease you'd put on the tracks? The war needed those. Some towns held scrap-metal drives on Halloween night instead.

And Chicago went further. In 1942, its city council voted to cancel Halloween for the rest of the war. [wry] Whether anybody actually told the kids is a different question.

Then the war ended. Sugar came back, and so did the soldiers. Then came the {baby boom}: millions of new kids, growing up in brand-new suburbs where the houses sat close together, every front door a few steps from the next. If you're five years old with a pillowcase, that's not a neighborhood. That's a buffet.

Candy companies noticed. Kids' magazines ran trick-or-treat stories. Even Donald Duck got a trick-or-treat cartoon. By the early 1950s, Halloween belonged to little kids.
