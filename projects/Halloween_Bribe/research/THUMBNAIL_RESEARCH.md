## Saturated look
- **Orange and black with a jack-o'-lantern.** Pumpkins appear in 7 of the 16 thumbnails. Orange sunsets, bonfires and full moons appear in most of the rest.
- **Samhain, druids and bonfires** for the "origins" angle: four or more recent videos (AI paintings, pencil sketches, real fire photos).
- **Dripping horror lettering** (yellow or red) and **cream or white serif "History of Halloween"** text.
- **AI-painted spooky folk figures** (straw masks, deer skulls, hooded ghosts), which has become common since 2025.
- **Title phrases that repeat:** "The History of Halloween", "The (ENTIRE/Real) History of Halloween", "Origins of Halloween", "How Samhain Became Halloween", "Dark Roots", "Strange/Weird Origins", "Bet You Didn't Know", "Why Do We Trick-or-Treat?".

## Gaps we can own
1. **Nobody uses the bribe or protection-money idea.** No title or thumbnail suggests that treats were a payoff to stop vandalism. Something like "Trick-or-Treating Was a Bribe" or "Candy Was Protection Money" doesn't exist yet.
2. **The 20th-century American story has little coverage.** Almost everything covers Celtic and medieval history. Only HISTORY's 1-minute short (8) shows 1930s vandalism, and only Crunchy History (15) covers pranks, as an AI grid of modern-looking scenes. The 1933 Bismarck tear gas, WWII sugar rationing and the 1974 Pasadena poisoning (Ronald Clark O'Bryan) don't appear in any top thumbnail or title.
3. **Visual contrast:** our field-notebook paper look with coral and teal (no orange-and-black, no pumpkin) would stand out on a results page that's almost all orange. Good subjects: a real 1930s newspaper clipping, a gas-mask or police image, or a candy bar paired with a broken window or overturned outhouse. Short text in a different color from the rest of the field ("A BRIBE.", "1933: TEAR GAS").
4. **A real-archival-photo look works.** HISTORY's burning-house vintage photo is the strongest image in the set. A real, cited period photo stands apart from the AI-painted wave.
