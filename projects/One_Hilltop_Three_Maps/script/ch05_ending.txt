So why do three religions from the same tiny region have such different maps?

Because they spread in different ways. Judaism, an ethnic religion, was scattered by exile and migration into a diaspora of small communities around the world. Christianity spread person to person, then through an empire, then across oceans with colonizers, until its center shifted south. And Islam spread through rulers and, above all, through traders, all the way to Indonesia.

But across both videos, one thing keeps pulling people back to these hearths: sacred spaces. Places like Varanasi, Bodh Gaya, Amritsar, Jerusalem, and Mecca draw millions of people every year on a pilgrimage, a journey to a holy place. And those journeys reshape the landscape around them, with roads, markets, hotels, and whole cities built to welcome the travelers.

Which is why this tiny walled city still matters to billions of people who will never live here.
