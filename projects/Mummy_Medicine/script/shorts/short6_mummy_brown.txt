There may be a painting in a museum near you with a person in it. Not painted on it. In it.

For centuries, artists bought a paint called {mummy brown}. It was made from exactly what it sounds like: ground-up Egyptian mummies.

Painters loved it for shadows. Most never asked what was in the tube.

One English painter found out over Sunday lunch. He got up, grabbed his tube, and buried it in the garden.

One London shop kept making it into the 1960s. Then they ran out of mummies.

And artists rarely wrote down which brown they used.

So next time you're in a museum, look closely at the shadows. And maybe say hi.
