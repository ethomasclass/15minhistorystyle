The Whigs read the insult, and they don't get mad. They hug it. Fine. Harrison is the log cabin candidate.

Suddenly, log cabins are everywhere. Little ones on parade floats. Full-size ones built at rallies. Cabins on wheels, pulled through town, with raccoon skins on the walls and a barrel of hard cider by the door. The raccoon becomes the Whigs' unofficial mascot. Don't ask.

Next, they go after Van Buren. A Whig congressman gives a very long speech about the "Royal Splendor" of the President's House. Gold spoons. Perfume. Fancy carpets. Rumor says he wears a corset. Most of it is exaggerated. Nobody checks.

So, voters, here's your choice. A hardworking man of the people in a log cabin. Or a perfumed aristocrat eating off gold. The man of the people grew up on a plantation. But sure.
