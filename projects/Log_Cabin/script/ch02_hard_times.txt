1837. Andrew Jackson leaves office and hands the country to his vice president, Martin Van Buren. Van Buren is a Democrat, Jackson's party. Same name as today's party, very different one.

Two months later, the economy falls apart. Banks close. Prices drop. People lose jobs, farms and savings. That sudden crash is called a {panic}, and this one turns into a {depression}, a slump that lasts for years.

Whose fault is it? If you're Van Buren's opponents, easy. He's the guy in the chair. They start calling him "Martin Van Ruin."

And who are his opponents? The Whigs. A new party, built to stop Andrew Jackson. The name comes from a British group that opposed kings, because they thought Jackson acted like one. We have a whole video on that.

The Whigs agree on exactly one thing: Van Buren has to go. On everything else, banks, taxes, roads, they argue like relatives at a wedding.

December 1839. They hold a {nominating convention}, a big meeting to choose their candidate, in Harrisburg, Pennsylvania. Their most famous leader, Henry Clay, wants the job. But Clay has made a lot of enemies. So the Whigs pick someone nobody hates.
