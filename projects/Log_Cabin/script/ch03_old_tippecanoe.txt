William Henry Harrison. Age 67. Former congressman, governor and general.

His big claim to fame is 1811. As governor of Indiana Territory, he marches an army toward a Native confederation's town, near the Tippecanoe River. The fight that follows is bloody. Harrison calls it a victory. His army lost a lot of men, and people argued about it even then. But it earns him a nickname: "Old Tippecanoe."

So what does Harrison promise the voters? Nothing. The Whigs don't even write a {platform}, a list of what the party plans to do. No list. No plans. His advisers mostly want him to keep quiet. And a candidate who never says what he'll do can never be blamed for what he didn't do.

That's a strategy. But it isn't a slogan. They need something to put on the banners.

Then the editor in Baltimore hands them one.
