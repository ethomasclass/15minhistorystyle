March 4th, 1841. Inauguration day. It's cold and windy. Harrison, now 68, rides to the Capitol on a white horse, with no overcoat and no hat. Then he gives the longest inaugural address ever. More than 8,000 words. Nearly two hours. Much of it is about ancient Rome.

The man who ran on a log cabin spends two hours on Rome.

One month later, April 4th, he's dead. That's 31 days. Still the shortest presidency in American history.

The story goes that he caught a cold that day and died of pneumonia. But doctors who've looked at his case more recently think it may have been typhoid fever, maybe from the White House water. So the long speech might be innocent. Nobody really knows.

No president had ever died in office before. The Constitution wasn't clear about whether the vice president became president, or just took over the job. John Tyler said: I'm the president. Critics called him "His Accidency." And Tyler, a former Democrat, soon vetoed the Whigs' bank bill. So the Whigs threw their own vice president out of the party. Within months.

So let's go back to the question. How did an insult about a log cabin get a man elected president?

The Whigs didn't argue with the insult. They wore it. Critics said it proved politics had turned into a costume party: loud, cheerful and empty. Supporters said it brought in millions of ordinary voters who'd never felt invited. Historians still argue about which. Both had a point. Eight in ten voters showed up, and plenty of them showed up for a raccoon.

Give him a barrel of hard cider, the editor said, and he'll sit in his log cabin. Instead, he got the White House. For 31 days.
