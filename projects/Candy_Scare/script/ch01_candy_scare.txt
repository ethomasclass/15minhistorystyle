An 8-year-old eats a Pixy Stix on Halloween night. Within an hour, he's dead.

His dad says it came from a dark house. The door opened a crack, and a hand reached out.

That hand is a big reason parents still check Halloween candy. So whose hand was it?

Outside Houston, 1974. Police find four more Pixy Stix in four other kids' bags. All full of cyanide. And the man at the dark house? He was at work.

The poisoner was *the dad*. He'd just bought life insurance on his kids.

He was caught. The fear wasn't. In 1982, someone in Chicago put cyanide in Tylenol. Seven people died. That Halloween, some towns banned trick-or-treating. Hospitals offered to X-ray candy.

So how many kids has a stranger's candy actually killed? A sociologist checked newspapers back to 1958. Hoaxes. A few pins. *Not one child.*

There was never a hand at the door. Just a dad, walking his kids from house to house.
