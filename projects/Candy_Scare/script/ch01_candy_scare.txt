An 8-year-old eats a Pixy Stix on Halloween night. Within an hour, he's dead.

His dad says it came from a dark house. Just a hand, reaching out the door.

That hand is a big reason parents still check Halloween candy. So whose hand was it?

Four more Pixy Stix turn up in other kids' bags. All laced with cyanide. And the dark house? The owner was at work.

The poisoner was *the dad*. He'd just bought life insurance on his kids.

He was caught. The fear wasn't. In 1982, someone poisoned Tylenol in Chicago. Seven people died. That Halloween, some towns banned trick-or-treating.

Forty years later, it was rainbow fentanyl: pills that looked like candy. Clinics still X-ray candy.

So how many kids has a stranger's candy killed? A sociologist has tracked the reports since 1958. Hoaxes. A few pins. *Not one child.*

There was never a hand at the door. Just a dad, walking his kids from house to house.
