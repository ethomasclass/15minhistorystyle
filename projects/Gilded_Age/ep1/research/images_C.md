# Archival images, set C: ch06 The Con Man's Son, ch07 The Trust Comes to Brooklyn, ch08 Leave It Alone

Downloaded with `find_images.py` (credits in `public/img/credits_C.json`). A few files were fetched by my own small helper
scripts that run the same licence test: `commons.py get` at 3840 px instead of the 1920 px default, and the LOC
`/pictures/item/<id>/?fo=json` record when the `loc.gov/item` record gave a 404 or a redirect loop (robber barons 1889,
Progress of the Century). Those two still required "No known restrictions on publication", and all their credits are in the
same JSON file. All paths below are relative to `ep1/`. 36 files: ch06 21, ch07 5, ch08 10.

Edits I made after download (the credits entries still apply): I rotated the sideways book plates upright and cropped
plates and portraits out of their book pages or microfilm borders. I made the two McClure's parents' portraits greyscale,
and cropped the colour bar off the LOC "Bosses of the Senate" and the museum credit line off the Dietz plates. Everything
else is as downloaded. Several items are colour (cartoons, labels, certificate): convert them to B&W in the edit.

## Gaps (beats with no usable archival image) and what would fill them

**ch06**
- **"sold bottles of homemade medicine that he said could cure almost anything. Even cancer."** No PD image of his own bottles or handbills. *Generated prop:* an 1850s patent-medicine bottle: small rectangular aqua blown glass about 5 in tall, with sunken side panels, a short neck, a cork stopper and a paper label wrapped round it. The label would be a hand-set wood-type broadside: "DR. W. A. ROCKEFELLER / THE CELEBRATED CANCER SPECIALIST / ALL CASES OF CANCER CURED." Tarbell quotes a handbill with that wording, so check it before using the exact words on screen. Optional pair: a small printed handbill in the same type.
- **"had a second wife under a fake name."** No image of Margaret Allen or of "Dr. William Levingston". *Drawn animation:* two calling cards or a hotel-register line, "Wm. A. Rockefeller" crossed out and "Dr. Wm. Levingston" written in (1850s copperplate hand).
- **"lent 50 dollars of his turkey money to a neighbor"**: turkeys and the loan. *Generated props:* a hand-made wooden turkey pen with a few bronze turkeys, and a paper IOU in a child's hand: "$50 at 7 per cent".
- **"wrote down every penny of his own in a little red notebook" (Ledger A, 1855)**: **no public-domain image.** The original is held by the Rockefeller family / Rockefeller Archive Center, and published photos are modern and copyrighted; none turned up on Commons, LOC or IA. *Generated prop:* a cheap pocket account book about 4 x 6 in, with a red marbled-paper or red cloth cover and a leather spine, bought for about 10 cents. It is open to ruled pages with red margin lines and dollars-and-cents money columns, and the entries are in brown-black iron-gall ink in a neat clerk's copperplate. Use only entries the research supports ("Sunday school .01"; small pay received, small sums spent and given) rather than invented lines.
- **"Hewitt & Tuttle" / the bookkeeping job**: not in the script by name; nothing found. A generated slant-top clerk's desk with ledgers and a quill or steel pen would cover it.
- **"seal oil cans with 39 drops of solder… instead of 40"**: no period photo of Standard's can-filling line. *Generated prop:* a 5-gallon export kerosene can of the 1870s–80s: a square-section tinplate can about 9.5 x 9.5 x 13.5 in with lapped, soldered seams and a small round screw cap or pour spout near one top corner. On the top centre is a soldered-on disc cap, and the solder beads shine as a ring of silvery drops round it (this is where the drops go). It would have a printed or stencilled paper label (cf. the Pratt's Astral Oil label in ch06). These cans were shipped two to a pine wooden case, so an open case with two cans is a good second prop. Animate a counter "40 → 39" over the bead ring.
- **"His secret weapon was a {rebate}… the railroads secretly paid him money back"** (and the South Improvement Co., 1872): no PD rebate cartoon or document image found. *Drawn animation:* a freight waybill and a returned cheque. The waybill reads "Lake Shore & Michigan Southern Ry. — 1,000 bbls refined oil, Cleveland to New York", and a smaller "drawback" cheque slides back to "Rockefeller, Andrews & Flagler" (the firm's name until 1870; "Standard Oil Co." after Jan. 1870). Use the Tarbell tank-car photos as the railroad background.
- **"he bought 22 of them" / "every pizza place in town"**: explainer graphics (already planned in ANIMATION_PLAN). The 1870 and 1889 Cleveland works photos make the before/after.
