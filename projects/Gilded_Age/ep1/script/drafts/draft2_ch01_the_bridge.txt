On the day the Brooklyn Bridge opened, a hundred and fifty thousand people walked across it. [serious] And some of the steel wire holding them up... had already *failed its safety test*.

Nobody in that crowd knew. Not even the nineteen-year-old who works ten-hour shifts in a sugar factory across the river. Call him Michael Kelly. [light] He's made up. But everything that happens to him really happened to people like him.

It's 1883. Twenty years earlier, this was a country of farms, fighting a civil war. Now it's hanging roads from steel cables, and making a few men richer than kings.

So here's the question. [curious] *How did America get so rich, so fast... and who ended up with the money?*

To answer it, we need two boys who started out poorer than Michael. One whose father lost his job to a machine. And one whose father was a con man.

And we'll come back to that bad wire. [thoughtful] It turns out to be the whole story, in miniature.
