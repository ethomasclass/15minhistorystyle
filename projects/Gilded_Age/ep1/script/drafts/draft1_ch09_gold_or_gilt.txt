[thoughtful] So let's go back to the bridge. And that bad wire.

Each of the bridge's four main cables isn't one giant rope. It's more than five thousand steel wires, bundled tight. Every wagonload of wire had to pass a strength test at the factory, and get a certificate, before it could go to the bridge.

The wire supplier found a way around that. A wagon of good wire would pass the test and get its certificate. Then, on the way to the bridge, it stopped at another building. The good wire came off. Wire that had *failed* the test went on. Same wagon. Same certificate. And the good wire went back to be tested again. And again.

They finally caught on because someone noticed the pile of rejected wire... [deadpan] never got any bigger.

By then, tons of bad wire were already inside the cables. Taking them apart meant starting over. So the chief engineer added 150 extra wires to every cable, and made the supplier pay for them. The bridge had been built to be six times stronger than it needed to be. With the bad wire, it was still about four or five times as strong.

It held. People walk across it every day.

[thoughtful] So, how did America get so rich, so fast? New machines made things cheap. Two poor boys figured out how to control whole industries, one by owning every step, and one by buying every rival. And a government that mostly left business alone let them do it.

And who ended up with the money? Carnegie and Rockefeller became two of the richest people who ever lived. Michael got a day's pay for a day's work, and went home to a kerosene lamp.

From the outside, the Gilded Age looked like solid gold. So did the bridge. But inside, some of it was cheap wire, holding on.

So that's the question this whole series is going to ask. [pause] *Who got the gold... and who only got the gilt?*
