There's one big word for what was happening: {industrialization}. Making things by machine, in factories, in huge numbers, instead of by hand.

Take steel. Iron is strong, but brittle. Hit it wrong, and it snaps. Steel bends instead. But making steel took a day or more, so it cost a fortune.

Then came the {Bessemer process}. Blow air through melted iron, and the air burns out the extra carbon. It takes minutes, not a day.

Steel rails dropped from more than 170 dollars a ton to about 17. *Same steel. One-tenth the price.* The steel frame inside every tall building you've ever been in starts right here.

Inventions were coming so fast, inventors were racing each other. Alexander Graham Bell's application for the telephone reached the patent office the same day as another inventor's notice for a very similar idea. Bell got the patent. History remembers one name.

And you've probably heard that Thomas Edison invented the light bulb. [pause] He didn't. Other inventors had working bulbs first.

What Edison made was a bulb that *lasted*, and the whole system around it: a power plant, wires under the street, and even a meter to measure how much electricity you used. [wry] So the next time someone in your house complains about the electric bill... that's Edison too.

New machines meant new factories. And new factories needed people. People like Michael.
