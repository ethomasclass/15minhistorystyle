Michael works at the Havemeyers and Elder sugar refinery, in Brooklyn, on the edge of the East River.

A refinery is a factory that cleans up a raw material. This one turns raw brown sugar into the white stuff in your kitchen. About a thousand men work here, making about 5,000 barrels of sugar a day.

The sugar is pumped to the top of the building and works its way down, floor by floor. Melted. Filtered through charcoal made from burned animal bones. [deadpan] Yes. Bones. Then boiled, and spun dry.

Michael works on one of the hottest floors. Ten hours or more a shift. If he doesn't work, he doesn't get paid. And there's always another man outside the gate who wants his job.

The main building is still standing on the Brooklyn waterfront today.

[thoughtful] The men who owned places like this were becoming some of the richest people in history. And the two richest of all started out poorer than Michael.
