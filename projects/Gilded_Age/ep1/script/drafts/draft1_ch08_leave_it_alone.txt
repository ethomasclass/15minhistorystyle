So where was the government in all this? Mostly... standing back.

The idea was called {laissez-faire}: French for "let them do it." Leave business alone. Let the {free market}, buyers and sellers, decide prices, wages, and who wins.

So there was no minimum wage. No limit on working hours. No safety rules. Nothing to stop one company from buying all its rivals. [dry] And no two-day weekend. People had to fight for that one.

But "leave it alone" wasn't the whole story. The government gave railroads about 130 million acres of land, more than all of California. And it taxed goods from other countries, so American factories had less competition.

Carnegie and Rockefeller were {entrepreneurs}: people who start a business and take the risk that it might fail. So were they heroes, or villains? People argued about it then. They still do.

To their fans, they were {captains of industry}. Builders. Steel got cheap. Lamp oil got cheap. Millions of new jobs, and America became the biggest industrial power in the world.

To their critics, they were {robber barons}. They crushed rivals with secret deals, paid low wages for long, dangerous days, and got rich off men like Michael.

Both sides were looking at the same facts.
