On the day the Brooklyn Bridge opened, a hundred and fifty thousand people walked across it. [serious] And some of the steel wire holding them up... had already *failed its safety test*.

Nobody in that crowd knew. It was May 1883. The longest suspension bridge in the world. Brass bands, fireworks, and a penny to walk across.

One of the people in that crowd is a nineteen-year-old named Michael Kelly. He works ten-hour shifts in the heat of a sugar factory, right across the river.

[light] Michael's made up. But everything that happens to him really happened to people like him.

Twenty years earlier, this was a country of farms, fighting a civil war. Now it's hanging roads from steel cables, lighting streets with electricity, and making a few men richer than kings.

So here's what I want to figure out. [curious] *How did America get so rich, so fast... and who ended up with the money?*

To answer that, we need two boys who started out poorer than Michael. One whose father lost his job to a machine. And one whose father was a con man.

And we'll come back to that bad wire. [thoughtful] It turns out to be the whole story, in miniature.
