First, the name. Because the name is an insult.

In 1873, Mark Twain and a friend wrote a novel making fun of greedy businessmen and crooked politicians. They called it *The Gilded Age*.

{Gilded} means covered in a thin layer of gold. Shiny outside, cheap metal underneath. [wry] If a "gold" necklace has ever turned your neck green, you've owned something gilded.

People later borrowed the name for the whole era, about 1870 to 1900. And things moved fast. Between the end of the Civil War and 1900, the country doubled, to about 76 million people. Railroads laid more than 200,000 miles of track.

They got so powerful, they changed time. Every town used to set its clocks by the sun, which made train schedules a nightmare. So in 1883, the railroads switched the country to a handful of time zones. [dry] Some towns heard noon twice that day.

Every time zone you've ever crossed? A railroad drew it.

And all those trains needed rails. Which were about to get very cheap.
