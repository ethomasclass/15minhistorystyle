First, though, this era has a name. And the name is an insult.

In 1873, Mark Twain and a friend wrote a novel making fun of greedy businessmen and crooked politicians. They called it *The Gilded Age*.

{Gilded} means covered in a thin layer of gold. Shiny on the outside. Cheap metal underneath. [wry] If you've ever had a "gold" necklace turn your neck green, you've owned something gilded.

Later, people borrowed the name for the whole era, from about 1870 to 1900. And it fit.

Because things were moving fast. The Civil War ended in 1865. By 1900, the country had doubled, from about 38 million people to about 76 million. Railroads laid more than 200,000 miles of track, and a trip across the country went from months to about a week.

The railroads got so powerful, they changed time. Before 1883, every town set its clocks by the sun, so noon in one town was a few minutes off from noon in the next. For train schedules, that was a nightmare.

So one November day, the railroads just switched the country to a handful of time zones. [dry] Some towns heard noon twice that day.

Every time zone you've ever crossed? The railroads drew it. The government didn't make it official for another 35 years.

And all those trains needed rails. Which were about to get very, very cheap.
