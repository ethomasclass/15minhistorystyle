# Episode 1: animation plan

How to put as much scene animation as possible into *Steel, Oil, and Sugar*, so it holds students and explains the ideas, built with
the look-dev kit (`../lookdev/`: `rig.js` for people, `scene.js` the bridge, `street.js` the street, `cutaway.js` the tenement).

## The idea in one paragraph

Animate **about three-quarters of the episode** and keep the rest on the desk with real photographs, documents and cartoons. The
animated scenes do two jobs: **story** (places and people moving, so the eye always has something alive to follow) and
**explanation** (the mechanism drawn moving: air burning carbon out of iron, a company owning every step, rivals going dark one by
one). The desk layer is where real faces live: Carnegie, Rockefeller, Edison and Bell appear as their real photographs, never as
drawn faces. That keeps the channel honest and gives students a rhythm: **scene, real photo, scene.**

## Rules that keep it engaging and honest

1. **Something changes every 4 to 6 seconds**: a camera move, a new action, a cut to the desk. No still holds longer than a sentence.
2. **Every new idea gets a moving picture of how it works**, shown at the moment the narrator says the word. The vocab card lands
   with it (index card or x-ray, orange handwriting).
3. **Home bases**: the bridge, the Kellys' street, the tenement, the refinery. Returning to them is a visual callback students can
   follow; Michael in coral is always the way back into the story.
4. **Real people, real photos.** The two men's childhoods have no photographs, so those scenes use **solid silhouettes with no face
   detail** (detail stays off even in close-ups) and a small mono tag the first time: `ANDREW CARNEGIE, AGE 12 · NO PHOTO`. Adults
   appear as archival photographs (cut-outs on the desk, or a photo pinned into a scene). The Kellys, who are composites, can have faces.
5. **Teal explains, coral is the Kellys.** In explainer animations, ownership and connections are drawn as teal traces and arrows
   (Carnegie's chain, Rockefeller's buyouts, the trust's strings), so coral keeps meaning "a Kelly."
6. **Motion matches the narration's words** (scenes timed to word timings, as the channel already does), and sound only for things
   on screen: steam, clicks, the sounder, the auction gavel.

Effort: **S** about an hour, **M** half a day, **L** a day or more.

## By the numbers

- **49 shots**: 7 already built, about 15 adapted from what exists, about 27 new; 6 archival or desk shots.
- **Animated share**: roughly 7 to 8 minutes of the 10.
- **The big builds**, in priority order: the vertical-integration scroll (27), the horizontal-integration scroll (38), the refinery
  (17), the Bessemer x-ray (11), the mills and looms set (10, 18, 19, 21). The first three are reused in later episodes (the
  refinery is Michael's workplace for the whole series; the trust strings return when the government answers the trusts).
- **New rig pieces needed**: a hand loom and power looms, spinning frames, a telegraph sounder, a converter, oil derrick and stills,
  a ledger, coins, a can lid with solder, ships on the Great Lakes, coke ovens. The figure rig already covers the people, including
  sitting, carrying, working and walking.

## Suggested build order

1. Prototype the two integration explainers (27 and 38) as a matched pair: the episode's teaching core, and the best test of
   whether animation explains better than a diagram.
2. The refinery (17), because Michael's story runs through it.
3. The Bessemer x-ray (11) and the mills (10, 18, 19, 21).
4. The small explainers (interest, ledger, solder, the wire fraud), which are quick and reuse the x-ray and desk styles.
5. Everything else adapts from sets that exist.
