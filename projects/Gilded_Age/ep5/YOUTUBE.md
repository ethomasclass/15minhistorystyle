## Description

4 a.m., July 6, 1892. Two barges full of armed guards drift up the river toward the Homestead steel mill. On the bank, thousands of workers are waiting. Nobody knows who will fire first.

In the finale of The Gilded Age, ordinary Americans fight back, and they do it three ways: at work, on the farm and at the ballot box. We follow the Kelly family of Brooklyn and Uncle Owen, a Nebraska farmer about to lose his land, through the rise of labor unions, collective bargaining and strikes, the Knights of Labor, the Haymarket affair and Samuel Gompers' AFL. We see Andrew Carnegie and Henry Clay Frick at Homestead: hero, villain, or both? Then the farmers' side: debt, railroads and falling prices, the Grange and the Farmers' Alliance, and the money problem explained simply (greenbacks, inflation, deflation, the gold standard and free silver). Plus the spoils system and Garfield's assassination, the Populist Party's Omaha Platform, the Panic of 1893, Coxey's Army marching on Washington, and William Jennings Bryan's Cross of Gold speech against McKinley in the election of 1896. Each door closes. But the ideas don't die.

The Kelly family is fictional. Everything that happens to them really happened to people like them.
