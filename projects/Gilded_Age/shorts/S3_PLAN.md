## Story arc

1. **Hook (0 to 5 s):** a staggering number and a mystery: nine million dollars, for one woman. (Accurate framing: the ball's aim
   was social acceptance by old money; Mrs. Astor did attend, per The Sun.)
2. **Escalation (5 to 22 s):** the craziest true details, one per beat: the costumes (cats, hornets; confirmed), the Electric Light dress
   with a battery-lit torch (Alice Vanderbilt), the hobby-horse quadrille down the stairs, supper at 2 a.m., the last dance at sunrise.
3. **The reveal (22 to 38 s):** why: new money vs old money; Alva vs Mrs. Astor; the calling-card legend told as "the story goes"
   (MCNY calls it apocryphal). Payoff line: "She came to the ball."
4. **The turn (38 to 52 s):** downstairs: the maid's whisper ("Don't drop it"), under four dollars a week, more than 1,300 years of pay.
5. **Out (52 to 58 s):** "The whole story is on Chunk History." End card (2 s).

## Facts (from ep2/RESEARCH.md fact checks)

- March 26, 1883; more than a thousand guests (MCNY 1,200; "more than a thousand" safe).
- Costumes (MCNY blog, from NYT/Sun): cat with taxidermied cat head and seven cat tails (Kate Fearing Strong); hornet with a diamond headdress (Lila Vanderbilt Webb); Mme. Le Diable, red satin, velvet demon, demon-head fringe (Bessie Webb); Duchess of Burgundy with real sapphires, rubies and emeralds (Edith Fish); Dresden quadrille "living porcelain dolls"; Louis XVIs, King Lear, Joan of Arc.
- Alice Vanderbilt as "The Electric Light," battery-lit torch (Ep 2 ch 1 and ch 3).
- Hobby-horse quadrille down the staircase (Ep 2 ch 3).
- Supper about 2 a.m.; final reel at sunrise (MCNY).
- Cost about $250,000 as reported; "roughly nine million today" (Ep 2's conversion).
- Calling-card story: legend (MCNY: apocryphal); Mrs. Astor and Carrie attended (The Sun).
- Maid pay under $4 a week; $250,000 / $187 a year ≈ 1,335 years ("more than 1,300 years").
