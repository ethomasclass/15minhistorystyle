In 1883, one family spent about nine million dollars, in today's money, on a single party. And the guest they cared about most was one woman.

It was a costume ball. More than a thousand guests came as kings, and cats, and hornets. One woman came as electricity. Her torch really lit up, wired to batteries hidden in her dress.

Dancers galloped down the grand staircase wearing fake horses around their waists. Supper was at two in the morning. The last dance ended at sunrise.

The host was Alva Vanderbilt. Her family was new money: rich, but not accepted. And the queen of old money, Mrs. Astor, had never once come to her house.

The story goes, Alva left Mrs. Astor's daughter off the guest list. So Mrs. Astor gave in, and left her calling card.

She came to the ball.

Downstairs, a maid carried the ice cream up from the cellar.

NORA: [whisper] Don't drop it. Don't drop it.

She earned less than four dollars a week. To pay for that one night, she'd have needed more than 1,300 years.
