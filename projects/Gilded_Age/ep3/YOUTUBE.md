## Title

Why Millions Came to America in the 1880s | The Gilded Age, Part 3

## Description

New York, 1884. A Jewish family from the Russian Empire steps off the boat at Castle Garden, and a stranger is already waiting for them. He knows their names. He says their father sent him.

Part 3 of The Gilded Age follows the Rabinowitz family from a small town in the Pale of Settlement to the Lower East Side. Why did millions of people leave everything behind? How did a family pay for the ticket, survive two weeks in steerage, and get past the runners waiting at the dock? We follow them through Castle Garden, into a crowded tenement and a sweatshop sewing clothes by the piece, and into a neighborhood where newcomers built their own banks, synagogues and aid societies. Along the way: the pogroms and the May Laws, "old" versus "new" immigrants, the Chinese Exclusion Act, the cartoonists who mocked the newcomers, and Emma Lazarus's poem for the Statue of Liberty.

The Rabinowitz and Kelly families are fictional. Everything that happens to them really happened to people like them.
