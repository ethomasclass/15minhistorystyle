Contact sheets: `research/sheets/<topic>.jpg`. Sources: Library of Congress ("no known restrictions on publication"), Wikimedia Commons (public domain), the Internet Archive (pre-1930 publications; National Archives microfilm), Chronicling America. Look-dev references for the drawn sets are separate (`REFERENCES.md`).

## Watch for

- **Keppler "Looking Backward":** the old `desk/keppler_looking_backward_1893_large.png` is only 1254x790 and soft. The new on-screen file is `g_nativism/keppler_looking_backward_puck_1893_ia_sim_puck_827.jpg`, stitched from the Internet Archive microfilm of *Puck* no. 827 (Jan 11, 1893, pages n7 and n8, each 3964x5141). The seam was aligned by matching the overlap; no visible join. Black and white (the print is colour). LoC's own scan (LC-USZ62-29010) is thumbnail-only outside the Library. Re-stitch from the IA pages for a 7300 px master if a deep zoom needs it.
- **Caricatures and slurs** are marked CARICATURE / SLUR (HANDOFF "Ep 3 addition"): brief, framed by narration, never played for laughs. Three are strongly offensive and should appear only if the script names what they show: `new_transatlantic_hebrew_line_1881`, `their_new_jerusalem_judge_1892` (both antisemitic) and `hamilton_where_the_blame_lies_judge_1891`. The Wasp's "A Statue for Our Harbor" stays out (decision of Nov 9).
- **Sensitive content:** pogrom engravings (mob violence), "Hobson's Choice" (Chinese men shot), The Wasp 1877 (assault), the c1907 Ellis medical photo (men stripped to the waist), Riis's poverty photos (children, people in bed). Riis framed his subjects as a reformer; narration should give them dignity.
- **Date labels needed** (LATE): anything after 1893: Ellis Island photos of 1902 to 1913, Stieglitz 1907, Hester Street c1902, Gompers c1902, the 1903 plaque, Puck 1894 and 1904, Red Star poster 1898. Earlier context: Castle Garden engravings of the 1860s and 1870s, Harper's 1869 steerage hold.
- **Real names:** the S.S. Russia list and Annie Moore's page name real people. Zoom on spelling, not on one family's story, unless the script is about them.
- **Small files** (under 1000 px on the long side) are marked with a small square below: insets or desk props, not full frame.
- Riis photos come from LoC prints of his work (most are 1536 px); the Museum of the City of New York holds the negatives (not free to use).

## Gaps

- Brody (1881 to 1882 refugee camp) and the Hamburg emigrant halls of the 1880s: no public-domain picture found (BallinStadt photos are 1900s).
- A Jewish family at the Hamburg or Bremen docks in the 1880s: none; the 1874 Harper's Hamburg steamer is the closest.
- An 1880s photograph of steerage berths: none (Harper's 1869 engraving or drawn).
- Annie Moore herself: no authentic photograph (the widely shared one is another woman). Use her list page and the 1892 building.
- Ellis Island chalk marks: no PD photo of chalk-marked coats found; the eye exam and medical line photos are 1902 to 1913.
- The first Ellis Island building: only King's Handbook (1280 px) and a 722 px photo. A larger King's scan was blocked (Commons rate limit); the IA copy of King's 1893 is a different edition.
- Williamsburg/Brooklyn streets in the 1880s: only a ferry stereo. Brooklyn Public Library holds more (not all PD).
- Letters home in Yiddish, prepaid ticket stubs, landsmanshaft society photos: none found PD for the period; use a drawn prop (`p05_letter_ticket`).
- Synagogues of the era (e.g. Eldridge Street, 1887): no period image found; HABS photos are modern.
- American Protective Association material beyond Bowers's portrait and Puck: none. Immigration Restriction League (founded 1894): outside our years, none taken.
- A naturalization certificate of the 1880s: only a later index card (Aarons, 1880). NARA naturalization images on Commons are 1900s to 1940s.
- Gompers, Lazarus manuscript: early Gompers photo not found; the manuscript is only 501 px (AJHS holds a larger scan).
- Not downloaded (rights "not evaluated" at LoC, though published before 1930): "In the steerage" (1891), Keppler photo 1880 (LoC copy), Lillian Wald, C. J. Taylor's "Mortar of assimilation" (LoC copy; a small Commons copy is used). Not digitized at LoC: Life's "And we open our arms to them" (1883), the Harrison restriction cartoon (1889), Gillam's "They are pretty safe there" (1882).
