On a Monday night in March, 1883, more than a thousand people came to a party on Fifth Avenue. Nobody was allowed in wearing ordinary clothes.

They came as kings. As queens. As cats, and hornets, and stars. One woman came dressed as electricity, holding up a torch that actually lit up, powered by batteries hidden in her dress.

Outside, a crowd packed the sidewalk just to watch them climb out of their carriages. The police had to push people back.

And downstairs, in the kitchen, an eighteen-year-old girl was carrying ice cream up from the cellar.

NORA: [whisper] Don't drop it. Don't drop it. Don't drop it.

That's Nora Kelly. Michael's little sister, from last time. She's made up too. But thousands of girls had her job, in houses just like this one.

The party reportedly cost about a quarter of a million dollars. Nora earned less than four dollars a week.

So here's what I want to know. *What did the richest people in America think they owed everyone else?*
