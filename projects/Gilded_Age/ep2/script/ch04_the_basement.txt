That night the Vanderbilts pulled cooks and servants from all their family's houses into one kitchen. The richest family in America threw the party of the century, and the supper came from the servants.

Nora was a {domestic servant}. And she was {live-in}: she slept in the house, in a small room near the roof. That meant she was on call almost all the time.

Most days she worked more than ten hours. Some days fourteen. She got one afternoon off a week, and part of Sunday.

Her pay was about three dollars and sixty cents a week, plus her bed and her meals.

So let's do the math.

A quarter of a million dollars, divided by what Nora earned in a year... comes to more than thirteen hundred years.

Nora would have had to start working before the Vikings reached North America to pay for one party.

On her afternoon off, Nora took the ferry home, to the same kind of room you saw last time.

MA: [dry] And how were the rich?

NORA: [tired] Tired. Like the rest of us. Only they slept till noon.

She put most of her wages on the table. Her mother and her little brother were sewing shirts by the lamp, paid by the piece. That money was how the family made rent.
