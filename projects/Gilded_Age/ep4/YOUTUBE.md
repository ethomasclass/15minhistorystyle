## Description

3 a.m., a tenement is burning in Brooklyn. Families stand in the snow with nothing. Then a man in a good coat arrives, and he is already handing out blankets, rooms and a hot meal. He asks for nothing. Not tonight.

Part 4 of The Gilded Age is about political machines: the organizations that ran American cities in the 1880s by trading favors for votes. Were they helping the poor or robbing them? We follow the Kelly family of Brooklyn through a city exploding in size, from skyscrapers and streetcars to crowded tenements, dirty streets and no safety net, and see how a machine really worked, from the precinct captain up to the boss. Along the way: Boss Tweed's courthouse, the $179,729.60 bill for forty chairs and three tables, the Thomas Nast cartoons that helped bring him down, the escape that ended when Spanish officials recognized him from a cartoon, and George Washington Plunkitt's famous line about "honest graft".

The Kelly family is fictional. Everything that happens to them really happened to people like them.
