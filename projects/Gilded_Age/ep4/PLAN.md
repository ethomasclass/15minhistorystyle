Episode question (the video may ask this one): **Did the political machines help the poor or rob them?** The series compelling
question is never named.

## Cold open (stands alone; no earlier episode needed)

**"The Fire" (recommended).** Winter night, 3 a.m., a tenement on fire. Families on the street in nightclothes, everything gone. The
fire engines come. But someone else arrives too, almost as fast: a man in a good coat. He finds them rooms for the night, pays the
rent, buys them clothes, food for a week. He asks for nothing. Not tonight.
Then the turn: the same organization that paid for those blankets had, a few years earlier, billed the city $179,729.60 for three
tables and forty chairs. [VERIFY: NYT 1871 Tweed Ring exposé figure] "So which was it? Were these men helping the poor... or robbing them?"
- Why it works: an emotional scene, a mystery (who is he, why does he help), a shocking number, and an open question that the episode
  pays off. Based on a real account: Tammany leader George Washington Plunkitt described going to fires and doing exactly this
  (Riordon, *Plunkitt of Tammany Hall*, 1905). [VERIFY wording; it is later than our 1880s setting, so narrate it as "how the machine worked"]
- Drawn set: night fire glow from off-screen (no flames on people), silhouettes on the street, the man in the good coat (no face).

Alternates:
- **"The Chairs."** Open cold on the receipt: $179,729.60 for 3 tables and 40 chairs, then the thermometers, the plasterer paid for
  "two days' work." Punchy and funny, but less emotional; better used as the turn inside "The Fire."
- **"The Man Who Was Caught by a Cartoon."** 1876, a ship docks in Spain; police arrest an American fugitive. They recognize him from a
  political cartoon. Who was he, and how did a drawing catch him? (Boss Tweed and Thomas Nast.) Great mystery; save it as the
  Tweed chapter's ending.

## Story spine (the Kellys, Brooklyn, mid-1880s)

- The younger Kelly brother (name TBD) is offered a city job through the machine and becomes a ward heeler: the human face of the
  machine, neither villain nor saint.
- Brooklyn had its own machine, Hugh McLaughlin's (not Tammany, until consolidation in 1898). Plan: the story stays in Brooklyn with
  "Brooklyn's own machine"; Tammany and Tweed (Manhattan) are the famous example across the river.
- Optional callback, one line only: the Rabinowitz father needs papers; the ward heeler offers help, "and remember us when you can
  vote." (Naturalization after 5 years' residence [VERIFY].)
