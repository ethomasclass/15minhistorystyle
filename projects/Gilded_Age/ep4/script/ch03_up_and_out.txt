Up came first.

Two things made it possible. Safety elevators, so nobody had to climb ten flights of stairs. Every day. With groceries. And frames of iron and steel, strong enough to hold the whole building up.

In 1885, Chicago's Home Insurance Building rose ten stories into the sky. Many people call it the first {skyscraper}.

Then, out. In 1883, the Brooklyn Bridge opened across the East River. Steam trains already rattled along elevated tracks above Manhattan's streets. And in 1888, in Richmond, Virginia, Frank Sprague got a whole system of electric streetcars running. Within a few years, cities all over the country had them.

That changed who lived where. If you could afford the fare and a house, you moved out along the streetcar lines, to quiet new neighborhoods: the first {suburbs}.

If you couldn't, you stayed in the crowded blocks, by the factories and the docks.

And those blocks were filling with more people than anyone could handle. Too many, too fast.
