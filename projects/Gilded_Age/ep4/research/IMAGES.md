# Episode 4 archival images

125 public-domain images for the edit, in `public/img/a_city_growth` to `j_reform`, with credits in `public/img/credits.json` (title, artist, date, licence, source page, source id). Contact sheets: `research/sheets/<topic>.jpg`. Sources: Library of Congress ("no known restrictions on publication"), Wikimedia Commons (public domain), the Internet Archive (pre-1930 publications: *Harper's Weekly* and *New-York Times* microfilm, Paine 1904, Riordon 1905, the 1871 *Times* pamphlet). Every file is a JPEG of at most 4000 px on the long side. Tools: `tools/find_images.py` (search, get), `tools/batch_get.py` (lists), `tools/sheet.py` (contact sheets), `tools/rename.py` and `tools/add_credit.py` (hand-made crops and stitches).

## Watch for

- **Key images for the script:** "Who Stole the People's Money?" (now a clean crop of the lower panel from the IA microfilm of *Harper's*, Aug 19, 1871), "The Brains", "The Tammany Tiger Loose" (stitched by hand from the two microfilm pages of Nov 11, 1871: the join is invisible in the art, but the caption loses "E." of "LOOSE" in the gutter), "As long as I count the votes", "Under the Thumb", "Tweed-le-dee and Tilden-dum" and its Oct 7, 1876 reprint with the capture story, and the *Times* front page of July 22, 1871 ("The Secret Accounts").
- **LoC copies that failed:** LoC items 2022641895 ("Who Stole") and 2021630656 ("Tiger Loose") returned 503 errors every time; the IA microfilm versions replace them. "Under the Thumb", "Too Thin!" and "The Power behind the Throne" are crops from Paine, *Th. Nast: His Period and His Pictures* (1904): fine for insets, soft at full frame.
- **Caricatures** are marked CARICATURE: brief, framed by narration, never played for laughs. Nast's "Move On!" (1871) caricatures both Irish voters and a Native American and should appear only if the script talks about who could vote. The 1856 Tammany naturalization scene draws the Irish as rough types. Puck's "Tammany Fagin" (an antisemitic Fagin figure) was downloaded and then deleted. Most of the Tweed cartoons caricature Tweed himself (a public figure), not an ethnic group.
- **Sensitive:** "The Tammany Tiger Loose" (allegorical violence, Columbia mauled, bodies in the arena); the 1860 tenement fire (the caption reports deaths; show the burning building only); the World Building fire (deadly, nothing shown); the Philadelphia fraud cover (a scuffle); the 1895 cable car "accident" (a fallen woman); Riis's lodging-room photo (people in bed). Nothing gory was kept.
- **Date labels needed** (LATE): Plunkitt (1905 book, c1910 portrait), the Tim Sullivan shoe line (1910), election crowds (1907), the 14th Street fire (1909), Seth Low (1901) and Parkhurst (1896), the 1890s skyline views, the Home Insurance postcard (1907) and photo (1920s). Plunkitt's fire story itself is from 1905: narrate it as "how the machine worked."
- **Real private people:** Riis's subjects, the men in the Bowery shoe line, the men and girl beside Plunkitt on the bootblack stand, the owner of "Elephant Johnnie's". The *Times* and pamphlet pages name Ring members and contractors (public figures for this story).
- **Small files** (under 1000 px on the long side) are marked with a small square below: insets only. The Home Insurance Building has no large period photograph on Commons or LoC; the 1885 lithograph (LoC 2010634573) is the best image of it.
- **Scans with frames or bars:** several LoC prints include mats, frames or colour bars (Home Insurance lithograph, the Cerberus drawing was trimmed). Crop in the edit.

## Gaps

- Electric trolley in Richmond (1888): no public-domain photo found; the only trolley images are 1897 and later (Boston, Rights not cleared at LoC).
- Cable cars on Brooklyn or Chicago streets in the 1880s: only the Brooklyn Bridge cable car machinery (1883) and the 1895 Broadway "accident" drawing.
- Horses, manure, dead horses in the street: no public-domain photo found for the 1880s (the famous ones are Municipal Archives / MCNY). Use the Opper 1879 cartoon or a drawn set.
- Street cleaning: Waring's "White Wings" (1895 onward) not found PD; after our date anyway.
- A burned-out family on the street at night: none found (and the plan keeps faces off screen). The cold open is better drawn; the 1860 and 1882 fire prints give the glow.
- Ward heeler, ward club or saloon interior in the 1880s: only "Elephant Johnnie's" (1876) and the Tim Sullivan shoe line (1910). No PD photo of a Tammany clubhouse interior.
- Hugh McLaughlin: only one engraved portrait (published 1910, 1196 px). No photograph of him or of Brooklyn's Democratic headquarters (Willoughby Street) found.
- Williamsburg in the 1880s: nothing usable; Brooklyn street photos are 1900 and later.
- Seth Low as Brooklyn mayor (1882 to 1885): only portraits from 1901 and later at usable size (an 1890 image is 494 px).
- Tweed Courthouse under construction (1860s): no photograph found; Valentine's 1868 lithograph shows the planned building.
- The Spanish arrest itself (Vigo, 1876): no image beyond Harper's reprint of the cartoon; the scene is drawn (`ep4_vigo`).
- Election day in Brooklyn in the 1880s: none; New York and Philadelphia scenes of the 1870s stand in.
- Not downloaded (rights "not evaluated" or blank at LoC, no Commons copy): Riis "Bootblacks, New York" (1896), "The East River, Brooklyn" (1900), the Leslie's 1883 bridge view (2006684386), the 1890 to 1920 horsecar and trolley photos (2005690145), the 1903 Chicago steel frame (89711517), the Harper's Monthly Tweed engraving (2004677360), Nast's "Even Tammany sachems draw the line" (Goldstein collection, restricted).
