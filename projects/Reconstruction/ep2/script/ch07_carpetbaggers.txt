Black voters weren't the only Republicans in the South. White Republicans got names too. Neither one was a compliment.

{Carpetbaggers} were Northerners who moved south after the war. The insult said they showed up with everything they owned in a cheap bag made of carpet, ready to fill it with whatever they could grab.

Some did come to get rich. But many were Union veterans, teachers, ministers and businessmen who stayed to rebuild. They held only a small share of offices, but some of the highest ones.

{Scalawags} were white Southerners who voted Republican. The word was slang for a worthless farm animal. Some were small farmers who had never wanted to leave the Union. Some were business owners who wanted railroads, schools and growth. And plenty of them still didn't believe in equality. They just believed in the Republican plan.

Here's one carpetbagger worth remembering. Adelbert Ames, a Union general from Maine, ran one of those military districts. Then Mississippi sent him to the U.S. Senate, in the same year as Revels.

And here's the twist. You might hear that Revels took Jefferson Davis's old seat. He didn't. Mississippi has two Senate seats. Revels got the other one. Davis's old seat went to Ames.

We'll see Ames again. He's going to be the one asking for help.
