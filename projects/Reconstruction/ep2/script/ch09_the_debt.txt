A crop takes all year to grow. And until harvest, a family has to eat.

So they bought what they needed on credit. Food, seed, clothes, tools. Usually from a local store, and the store was often owned by the planter.

To get that credit, they signed a {crop lien}. That's a promise that the store gets paid first, out of the crop, before the family sees a cent.

The prices were high. So was the interest. And at the end of the year came settling up. Here's what that looked like.

The crop is sold. The family's half comes in. Then the store subtracts everything they bought, plus interest. And the family finds out it owes more than it made.

That means they can't leave. In many places, leaving with a debt could get you arrested. So they sign on for another year, already behind. Then another.

Some people called it slavery by another name. It wasn't slavery. You couldn't be sold. Your family couldn't be split up. But for a lot of families, it was a trap with no way out.

And remember those six words in the 13th Amendment? *Except as a punishment for crime.*

Southern states started leasing out prisoners. Railroads, mines and planters paid the state, and the prisoners did the work, unpaid. Most of them were Black, many arrested for things like vagrancy, the crime of not having a job. Thousands died doing it.

It's called {convict leasing}. And it was legal.
