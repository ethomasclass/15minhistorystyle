Here's where we left the wall. The Civil War ended, and slavery ended with it. President Andrew Johnson pardoned the Confederacy's leaders and gave them back their land. The South answered with the Black Codes. And Congress fought back with the 14th Amendment. When the freed families of Edisto Island lost their land, they described themselves in three words. *Landless. Homeless. Voteless.*

By 1867, Congress was in charge. And it was done asking nicely.

In March, it passed the {Reconstruction Act}, over Johnson's veto. Here's what it did, in three parts.

One. Ten Southern states were put under the army. They were split into five {military districts}, each run by a Union general. Tennessee was left out. It had already ratified the 14th Amendment.

Two. To get out, each state had to write a new constitution. And Black men had to be allowed to vote, both for the people writing it and in every election after.

Three. Each state had to ratify the 14th Amendment.

This is the start of {Congressional Reconstruction}. Some people call it Radical Reconstruction.

To many white Southerners, it was a military occupation. They called it rule by bayonet. To freedpeople, it was something brand new: a government with soldiers, standing on their side.
