Johnson couldn't stop the Reconstruction Act. So he did the next best thing. He got in its way.

He removed army generals who enforced it too hard. And Congress saw that coming. It passed a law called the {Tenure of Office Act}. It said the President couldn't fire certain top officials without the Senate's permission.

The official they were protecting was Edwin Stanton, the Secretary of War. He ran the army, and he was the last Radical ally in Johnson's cabinet.

In February 1868, Johnson fired him anyway.

Three days later, the House voted to {impeach} him. Impeaching is like filing charges. The House accuses; then the Senate holds a trial. And it takes two-thirds of the Senate to remove a president.

One of the men who argued the case against him was Thaddeus Stevens. Remember him? The Radical with the land plan. He was so sick by then he had to be carried in.

In May, the Senate voted. 35 to remove Johnson. 19 to keep him. One vote short of two-thirds.

Why? Some Republicans thought firing a cabinet member wasn't the kind of crime the Constitution meant. Others were nervous about who would replace him. Either way, Johnson stayed. But he had almost no power left.

Stevens died that summer. He asked to be buried in one of the only cemeteries in his town that accepted Black and white people alike. His gravestone says he chose it to show, in death, "equality of man before his Creator."

His plan to give freedpeople land died with him.
