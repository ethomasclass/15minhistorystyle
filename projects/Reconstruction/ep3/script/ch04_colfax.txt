In 1872, Louisiana had an election so disputed that both sides claimed to have won the state. And the next spring, in the small town of Colfax, that fight turned deadly.

Easter Sunday, 1873. Black men, some of them Union veterans, were guarding the courthouse for the Republican officials. A white militia surrounded them, with a cannon.

The courthouse was set on fire. Men who surrendered were shot. How many died? We don't know exactly. At least 60 Black men. Possibly more than 150.

It's called the {Colfax Massacre}.

Federal prosecutors charged some of the attackers under the Enforcement Acts. The case went all the way to the Supreme Court.

And in 1876, the Court threw the convictions out. Its reasoning was this: the 14th Amendment stops *states* from denying people equal protection. It doesn't cover private citizens attacking other private citizens.

That means it was up to the state of Louisiana to punish the mob. The same state where the mob had won.

Nobody was ever punished for Colfax.
