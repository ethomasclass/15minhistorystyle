Last time: Congress sent the army back into the South. Black men voted, and about 2,000 of them held office. The 15th Amendment wrote the vote into the Constitution. But most freedpeople still didn't own land. And the whole thing rested on two things: votes, and the soldiers who protected them.

Somebody had already figured out how to attack both.

In 1866, six Confederate veterans in Pulaski, Tennessee, started a club. They gave it a strange name: the {Ku Klux Klan}. Within a couple of years, it had spread across the South.

They rode at night, in masks and robes. They whipped, burned and killed. Their targets were Black voters, Black officeholders, teachers at Black schools, churches, and white Republicans too.

Night mattered. Under slavery, white patrols had controlled the roads after dark. Since freedom, Black Southerners had used the night for church meetings, political clubs, and visiting family. The Klan wanted the night back.

And the goal was simple. Keep Black men from voting, and the Republican governments would fall.

This was terrorism. Violence meant to frighten a whole group of people into giving up their rights. And it worked, at first.
