Then the federal government hit back.

Between 1870 and 1871, Congress passed a series of {Enforcement Acts}. The biggest is often called the Ku Klux Klan Act. They made it a federal crime to use force to stop someone from voting, and they let the President use the army against it.

President Grant used it. In October 1871, he declared nine counties in South Carolina to be in rebellion. He suspended {habeas corpus} there. That's the right not to be held in jail without being charged with a crime. Suspending it meant the army could arrest suspects and hold them.

Soldiers and federal marshals arrested hundreds of men. Hundreds more turned themselves in and confessed. Federal courts held trials.

And the Klan, as an organization, was largely broken.

Hold onto that. Because it proves something. When the federal government decided to protect Black voters with real force, it worked.

On the card, *protection* gets its check mark.

The question was whether the government would keep deciding that.
