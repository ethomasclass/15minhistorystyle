And then there was the bank.

In 1865, Congress created the {Freedman's Savings Bank}, a bank for freedpeople. Many depositors believed the government stood behind it. It didn't.

Tens of thousands of people opened accounts. Soldiers saved their pay. Churches saved. Families put away a few dollars at a time, hoping to buy a farm someday. Forty acres, maybe.

But the men running the bank made risky loans, some of them to their friends.

In 1874, with the bank near collapse, its trustees brought in the most famous Black man in America, Frederick Douglass, to be its president. He even put in 10,000 dollars of his own money.

It was too late. Within months, the bank closed. More than 60,000 depositors were owed almost 3 million dollars.

The government didn't make them whole. Over the years, only about half of what they were owed was ever paid back. Many people never saw a cent.

For a lot of families, that was their savings, and their trust, gone at once.
