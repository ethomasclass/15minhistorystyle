Meanwhile, up North, people had other things on their minds.

In 1873, one of the country's biggest banks collapsed, and it took the economy down with it. It's called the {Panic of 1873}. A depression followed that lasted for years. Businesses failed. Workers lost their jobs.

When you're worried about your own paycheck, a fight in Louisiana feels very far away.

Then there were the scandals. Grant was honest himself, but people around him weren't. The worst was the {Whiskey Ring}. Government tax officials let whiskey makers skip their taxes, in exchange for a cut. One of the men charged was the President's own private secretary.

Reformers who had once fought slavery now turned to a new cause: cleaning up the government. Black rights slid down their list.

In 1874, voters gave Democrats control of the House for the first time since before the war.

Republicans managed one last law on their way out, the {Civil Rights Act of 1875}. It banned discrimination in hotels, theaters and trains. Almost nobody enforced it. The Supreme Court struck it down eight years later.

More and more Northerners talked about the South as a problem they were tired of solving.
