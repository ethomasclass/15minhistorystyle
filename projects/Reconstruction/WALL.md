# The wall: one evidence board for the whole series

> **Look chosen:** the chunk wall, where every chunk is a block *in* the wall (mockups `review/wall_mockups/E*`).
> It's now a channel-wide device: see [`../../techniques/chunk_wall.md`](../../techniques/chunk_wall.md). The card
> descriptions below still hold; read "card" as "block".

Every chapter adds one **chunk** to a single evidence wall. Each episode ends by pulling back to show its chunks in
place, and the last minute of the series pulls all the way back to the whole wall. That's the *Chunk History* idea on
screen: small pieces you can hold, adding up to the big picture.

This page is the plan for building it. Chapter scripts point at it in their production notes; the narration says
"wall" only at a few places, listed below, so the words never fight the picture.

## The board

- **One huge desk-wall**, dark paper with the faint ruling, seen in perspective. It never resets between episodes:
  Episode 2 opens on Episode 1's wall, and Episode 3 on Episodes 1 and 2.
- **Three columns** that appear only when the story earns them:
  - **LAW** (appears in Ep 1 ch06, with the 13th Amendment)
  - **LAND** (appears in Ep 1 ch02, with Field Order 15)
  - **PROTECTION** (appears in Ep 2 ch02, with the military districts)

  The column names are the series answer, so the viewer watches the answer get built instead of being told it.
- **Top centre: the Edisto petition**, pinned first, in the first minute of the series. Its three words (*Landless.
  Homeless. Voteless.*) change over the series: VOTELESS is crossed out in Ep 2 and written back in in Ep 3.
- **Cards are chunks.** Each card is a cream slab with the Chunk History brand's notched corners, holding one
  archival image or document crop and a two-to-four-word label in Abril. The chunk for the chapter's subject gets the
  coral tint; everything else stays black and white.
- **Strings are causes.** A teal string with a small handwritten label ("therefore", "but") joins a cause to its effect,
  drawn on as the narration says the link. Dashed string = suspected, not proven (the 1877 deal).
- **Pins land on the chapter's last line**, with a soft knock (the brand intro's knock, not a stamp).

## The five new techniques, and where each one lives

| Technique | Where | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence wall** | All three episodes; zoom-outs at every episode's end | The chunks adding up |
| **Evidence wall, close-up investigation** | Ep 1 ch04 (the Booth plot), Ep 3 ch08 (1877) | Portraits strung together: three targets, one night; the dashed string to "the deal" with a "no proof" tag |
| **Powers-of-ten zoom** | Ep 1 ch02 (the South → the coast strip → Edisto → one 40-acre plot, "about 30 football fields"); reused in Ep 2 ch08 (the same plot, now with a planter's name on it) | Scale made physical |
| **Testimony as a transcript** | Ep 3 ch01 and ch07 | Margaret Ann Caldwell's Q-and-A on the printed Senate page, lines highlighted as they're read |
| **Telegrams as texts** | Ep 3 ch07 (Ames → Grant; the attorney general's reply quoting the President) | Real telegram slips, stacking like a message thread |
| **One big canvas** | Ep 1 ch07–ch09 (Bureau veto → override → 14th Amendment → Tennessee → the tour → the election) | No cuts: the camera travels along the wall from card to card as the fight escalates |

## Where the narration says "wall"

Only five places, so it stays a picture, not a gimmick:

1. Ep 1 ch10: "So let's step back, look at the whole wall, and go back to the question."
2. Ep 2 ch02: "Here's where we left the wall."
3. Ep 2 ch10: "So let's step back, look at the wall, and go back to the question."
4. Ep 3 ch02: "Here's the wall so far."
5. Ep 3 ch10: "So step all the way back and look at the whole wall."

Ep 1 has no recap: its wall starts empty, with the petition.

Everything else is shown, not said: cards land on the lines in the tables above.

## Other script changes in this pass

- Ep 1 ch02: "That's about 30 football fields." (40 acres ≈ 30.3 American football fields with end zones), for the
  powers-of-ten zoom.
- Ep 3 ch01: the testimony's own Q-and-A ("When did he die?" "Thursday night, in the Christmas.") so the transcript
  treatment has real lines to show.
- Ep 3 ch07: Ames "telegraphed" Grant, and the reply is attributed correctly: the attorney general's letter
  quoting the President (the "tired out" words are Grant's). Check against the letter before voicing.

## Brand

The *Chunk History* assets live on the style repo's branch `ccr-7a3e82c2-jvydrp`, in `brand/chunk-history/`: the
brand sheet, the "Quarter past" mark and "Two slabs" wordmark (SVG, teal / coral / ink / cream), an 8.7 s 3D intro set to
the channel jingle (`final/chunk-intro.mp4`, source in `intro/`), and three patterns (`pattern-chunks.svg`,
`pattern-cracks.svg`, `pattern-dials.svg`). The wall's cards take the slab shape and notched corners from that mark, so
the wall and the logo read as one system. Not merged into this branch yet.
