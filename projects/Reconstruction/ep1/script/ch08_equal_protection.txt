But a law can be repealed by the next Congress, or struck down by a court. If these rights were going to last, they had to go somewhere much harder to undo. The Constitution.

Meanwhile, every Johnson veto pushed more moderate Republicans over to the Radicals' side.

So in June 1866, Congress sent the states the {14th Amendment}. Its first section does three big things.

One. Anyone born in the United States is a citizen. That overturned the {Dred Scott decision}, an 1857 Supreme Court ruling that Black Americans could never be citizens.

Two. No state can take away anyone's life, liberty or property without a fair legal process. That's {due process}.

Three. No state can deny anyone "the equal protection of the laws." {Equal protection}.

Translation: a state can't have one set of laws for white people and another for Black people.

Sound familiar? That's aimed straight at the Black Codes. *The Black Codes are why this amendment exists.*

The states still had to ratify it. So Johnson told the Southern states to vote it down. And they did. All of them except one: Tennessee. Johnson's home state.

Which must have made for an awkward trip home.
