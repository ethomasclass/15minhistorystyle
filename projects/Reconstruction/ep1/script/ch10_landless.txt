So let's go back to the question. The war was over. Slavery was over. Who got to decide what freedom would look like?

At first, the President. Lincoln planned to be generous, to win the South back fast. Johnson was even more generous to the men who had led it. He pardoned them and gave back their land.

The South used that freedom to pass the Black Codes. That pushed Congress to fight back with the Civil Rights Act and the 14th Amendment. And when Johnson fought that, voters handed Congress control.

Johnson's supporters saw it differently. To them, he was following the Constitution: let the states run their own affairs, and heal the country fast. To the Radicals and to freedpeople, he had handed the South right back to the men who had led it out of the Union.

And Edisto Island? Most of the freed families there lost the land.

Remember their three words? *Landless. Homeless. Voteless.*

By the end of 1866, they had something new. The law. Slavery was gone from the Constitution, and the 14th Amendment was on its way.

What they still didn't have was the land.

But one of those three words was about to change.
