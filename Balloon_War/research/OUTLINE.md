## Driving question
**How do you fight a war with air?** Three balloons, three jobs: one protected, one lied, one killed.

## Chapter beats

### 1 · Look up (cold open)
- Concrete open: the 2023 balloon (sourced in 02/03). "For a week, the whole country was looking up."
- Turn: in World War Two, balloons did three jobs. One protected soldiers. One lied to an army. And one crossed an
  ocean and killed six people in Oregon, and almost nobody was told.
- Question: "So how do you fight a war with air?"
- Hook into ch2: "It starts on a beach in France, with a balloon on a leash."

### 2 · The protector
- What a barrage balloon is, plainly: a balloon on a steel cable. Planes that dive low hit the cable. So enemy pilots
  stay high, where it's hard to aim.
- D-Day, June 6, 1944 (the one date): soldiers carried small balloons, about 35 feet long, onto the beaches by hand.
- The men holding the cables: the 320th, about 600 Black soldiers, the only all-Black combat unit on the beaches.
  (Exact wording: not the only Black troops; about 2,000 landed that day.)
- Woodson, a medic: wounded before he reached the sand, treated about 200 men over about 30 hours. (Name him: the
  viewer meets him again in 2024.)
- The other half of the story: at home in Tennessee, they watched German prisoners of war served in restaurants that
  wouldn't serve them. Fighting for a country that segregated you. (Double V in one sentence, APUSH.)
- Today line: Woodson got the Distinguished Service Cross in 2024, eighty years later. A general laid it in the sand
  at Omaha Beach. (Re-check his Medal of Honor status before voicing.)

### 3 · The fake (humor chapter)
- "The US Army had a unit whose weapons were inflatable tanks." 93 pounds. About 1,100 men; many were artists.
- What they did: rubber tanks, speakers playing recorded tank engines, fake radio chatter, fake patches, gossip in
  cafes. 21 big fake operations.
- The story: a few French cyclists saw four soldiers pick up a tank. (Told as one veteran's memory: "The Americans
  are very strong.")
- One artist read Vogue in his foxhole. His name, Bill Blass, later went on fashion labels (only if it lands; else cut).
- It worked well enough to stay secret for more than forty years. Congressional Gold Medal, 2024.
- Today line: inflatable decoys are still made; a modern maker wants the enemy to waste a missile worth four times more.
- Mid-video subscribe, with a wry turn ("unlike the Ghost Army, I'm a real channel").

### 4 · The paper balloon
- Pivot: "On the other side of the world, Japan had a different idea."
- How: paper balloons about 33 feet wide, glued from small sheets with a paste made from a root vegetable. Hungry
  workers ate the paste. Made mostly by schoolgirls who were never told what for, in theaters and a sumo hall.
- The trick: a high, fast river of wind. Sandbags drop when it sinks, so it bobs across the Pacific in about three days.
  Then it drops its bombs.
- About 9,300 launched. About 300 found, from Alaska to Mexico to Michigan.
- Today line: the jet stream and your flight times.

### 5 · Silence
- They landed, and newspapers said nothing. The government asked them not to, so Japan wouldn't know they worked.
- It worked: Japan heard nothing and gave up in April 1945.
- One shut down power at Hanford, where the US was making material for the atomic bomb. (One clause; no detail needed.)
- The cost of silence: "But nobody warned the people who lived where the balloons came down."

### 6 · Bly, Oregon *(heavy)*
- May 5, 1945. A pastor's wife, Elsie Mitchell, pregnant, and five children on a Sunday-school picnic in the woods.
- They found something in the trees. It exploded. All six died. The pastor survived. (Five children, ages about 11 to 14.)
- The only people killed by enemy action on the US mainland in the whole war.
- After, the government finally warned the public.
- Today line: some are still out there (2014, British Columbia).

### 7 · The answer and the cranes
- Back to the question: three balloons. One protected men the country wouldn't treat as equals. One won battles by
  lying. One killed a family on a picnic and was hidden.
- The cranes: 1987, the women who made those balloons as schoolgirls folded a thousand paper cranes for Bly, carried
  there by a Japanese American man who as a boy had been held in a camp about fifty miles away. (Say "regret", not
  "apology".)
- Closing line, candidates: "A war that started with paper balloons ended, for one small town, with paper birds." /
  "Same paper. Different job."

## Handle with care (from the research)
- **Don't say:** "declassified in 1996"; "saved 15–30,000 lives"; "sand detectives stopped the attacks"; "germ
  balloons"; "the only Black soldiers at D-Day"; "Dabney, the last survivor".
- **Tell as memory:** the four-men-lift-a-tank story.
- **Conflicting numbers:** balloon flying height at Normandy (200 ft vs under 2,000 ft): avoid a number; Bly ages: say
  "five children"; time the 320th stayed in France: avoid a number.
- **Re-check right before voicing:** Woodson's Medal of Honor status; the 320th Congressional Gold Medal bill.
- **The 2023 balloon:** facts only (what, when, where it was shot down). No politicians, no blame (objectivity rules).
