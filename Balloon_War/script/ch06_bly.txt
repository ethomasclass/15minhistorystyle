May 5, 1945. The woods near a small town called Bly, Oregon.

A pastor named Archie Mitchell drove his wife, Elsie, and five children from their church up into the mountains for a picnic. Elsie was pregnant.

While Archie parked the car, the others walked ahead. The children found something strange in the trees, and they called him over.

He shouted a warning. It was too late.

It was one of the balloons. Elsie and all five children were killed. The children were between about 11 and 14 years old.

They were the only people killed by enemy action on the US mainland in the entire war.

A few weeks later, the government finally warned the public about the balloons.

And some of them are still out there. In 2014, two forestry workers in British Columbia found one, half buried in the ground. A military bomb team had to blow it up.

About forty years after the picnic, a man arrived in Bly carrying a gift from Japan.
