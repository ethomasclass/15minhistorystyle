## Answer Key (teacher)

1. No painting or photograph of him exists; page 2 is a written description (age, height, broad shoulders, large eyes, "walks brisk and active").
2. They voted no, and then they made slavery harsher.
3. …hold on tighter than ever?
4. Tobacco was wearing out the soil; many planters were switching to wheat, which needed far fewer workers.
5. Slowly starve.
6. About 1 pound; about 50 pounds.
7. Cleaning got easy, so planters wanted to grow as much cotton as possible, and somebody still had to plant it, hoe it and pick every boll by hand.
8. The Louisiana Purchase (the Mississippi Valley), and the Indian Removal Act of 1830, which forced the Cherokee, Creek, Choctaw, Chickasaw and Seminole nations off their homelands.
9. Oil. Any one of: Northern banks loaned the money, Northern ships carried the bales, Northern mills spun the thread.
10. It made them more valuable.
11. Selling people from the Upper South to the Deep South; roughly a million people.
12. The law didn't recognize enslaved people's marriages, so nothing stopped an owner from selling a husband, a wife or a child.
13. Money.
14. About three out of every four.
15. Status: slavery guaranteed there was always someone below them. "Being white meant being free." (After Southampton, also fear.)
16. Each person's cotton was weighed. Fall short: you could be whipped. Hit your number: your number might go up.
17. That was exactly why he had to learn.
18. Property; absolute.
19. In the eyes of the court, Celia wasn't a woman defending herself; she was property that had turned on its owner. (The judge wouldn't let the jury consider the "any woman" law.)
20. Any two of: married anyway; named children after grandparents, aunts and uncles to keep family ties; family as "a world outside of the world of work"; a faith of their own (Moses, a God who sets his people free); spirituals; hush harbors.
21. Any two of: working slowly, breaking tools, faking sickness, running away. Jacobs hid in a crawl space in her grandmother's attic for almost seven years, then escaped north in 1842 and later wrote *Incidents in the Life of a Slave Girl*.
22. The eclipse: the moon slid in front of the sun in the middle of the day and the sky went strange and dim.
23. A white lawyer, Thomas Gray, interviewed Turner and chose what to print.
24. Any two of: Virginia banned Black preachers from holding meetings; tighter slave codes with curfews, travel passes and bans on gatherings; more laws against teaching enslaved people to read; more patrols. (Calhoun called slavery "a positive good.")
25. More than all the country's railroads and factories combined.
26. Money (enslaved people were the biggest pile of wealth in the country, the engine of the most valuable export), and status (slavery "held up the whole pyramid" and told even the poorest white farmer who he was). After Turner: fear.
